# Immersion Activities Reference: Comprehensive Designs by CEFR Level

This reference document provides detailed activity designs for immersion-based language learning, organized by CEFR level and skill type. Use these designs to create tailored materials that engage learners in authentic-feeling contexts.

---

## A1 Level (Beginner) Immersion Activities

### Reading: Personal Stories About Daily Life

**Activity**: "A Day in the Life" Interlinear Reading Passage

**Objective**:
- Comprehend present-tense narratives about familiar daily routines
- Build recognition of high-frequency verbs and time expressions
- Begin reading without full translation dependency

**Design**:
- Length: 60–80 words
- Structure: Morning routine (wake up → breakfast → school/work → evening → sleep)
- Vocabulary: Core 500 words only; all verbs regular or most common irregular
- Format: Interlinear (target language + word-by-word gloss + natural translation)

**Example Structure (Spanish)**:
```
Mi rutina diaria (My Daily Routine)

Por la mañana
By  the morning
In the morning

Me levanto a las siete.
Self I-rise-up at the seven
I wake up at seven.

Desayuno con mi familia.
I-breakfast with my family
I have breakfast with my family.

[Continue through day...]

Vocabulario (Vocabulary):
- levantarse: to wake up
- desayunar: to have breakfast
- acostarse: to go to bed

Preguntas de Comprensión (Comprehension Questions) - Answer in English:
1. What time does the narrator wake up?
2. What does he/she do before lunch?
3. When does he/she go to bed?
```

**Comprehension Support**:
- 3–4 simple factual questions (answerable by pointing to text)
- Picture support (optional but valuable)
- Vocabulary glossary with 5–8 key words

**Cultural Note**: Include brief note on typical daily schedules in target culture (e.g., Spanish siesta tradition, European meal times).

### Conversation: Simple Service Interactions

**Activity**: "At the Café" Scenario Card

**Objective**:
- Practice ordering food and drinks in controlled context
- Learn polite phrases and basic numerical expressions
- Build confidence in real-world service interactions

**Design**:

```
SCENARIO: Ordering at a Café

Situation:
You enter a small café in [target culture]. You want to order a coffee and a pastry.
You have a few coins and aren't sure of the price.

Key Vocabulary (with pronunciation):
- café (cah-FEH): coffee
- agua (AH-wah): water
- pan (pahn): bread
- pastry: pastel (pahs-TELL)
- cuánto cuesta (KWAN-toh KWES-tah): how much does it cost?
- por favor (por fah-VOR): please
- gracias (GRAH-see-ahs): thank you

Useful Expressions:
GREETING THE STAFF:
- "Hola" (Hello)
- "Buenos días" (Good morning - before 12pm)

ORDERING:
- "Quiero un café, por favor" (I want a coffee, please)
- "Un café y un pastry, por favor" (A coffee and a pastry, please)
- "¿Cuánto cuesta?" (How much is it?)

POLITE CLOSINGS:
- "Gracias" (Thank you)
- "Hasta luego" (See you later)

Cultural Context:
- In [target culture], it's polite to say "Buenos días" before noon, "Buenas tardes" after
- Service is often slower than in English-speaking countries; this is normal and not rude
- Tipping customs: [explain typical tipping in target culture]

ROLE A (You - The Customer):
Your goal: Order a coffee and ask the price
Opening: Walk up and say "Hola"
Key points: Ask politely, listen for the price, thank them
Challenges: You don't understand the price clearly; ask them to repeat

ROLE B (Friend - The Café Staff):
Your goal: Take their order and tell them the price
Opening: Greet them and ask what they want
Key points: Be patient, repeat prices clearly, thank them for their order
Challenges: They ask for something not on the menu; suggest an alternative

CONVERSATION FLOW SUGGESTION:
1. Greet each other
2. Customer asks what's available
3. Staff suggests popular items
4. Customer orders coffee and pastry
5. Staff states price
6. Customer asks for clarification (to practice listening)
7. Both exchange thanks and say goodbye

Common Mistakes to Avoid:
- Don't say "lo quiero" (I want it) without the object—say "quiero un café"
- Don't forget "por favor" (it's seen as rude without it)
- Don't assume the server will speak English; practice in the target language

FOLLOW-UP DISCUSSION:
1. In your country, how do you order at a café?
2. What's the most expensive item you'd order? Practice ordering it.
3. How would you ask for different items (tea instead of coffee)?
4. Role-play: You're paying but forgot the word for the item. How do you ask?
```

**Variations**:
- At a bakery (asking for bread, quantities)
- At a bookshop (asking for a book, checking if in stock)
- At a simple market (purchasing single items, bargaining in appropriate cultures)

### Grammar: Present Tense of Regular Verbs

**Activity**: Reference Sheet and Practice Exercises

**Design**:

```
GRAMMAR FOCUS: Present Tense - Regular -AR Verbs (Spanish)

PATTERN (Presented inductively):

The verb hablar (to speak):

I speak     →  Yo hablo
You speak   →  Tú hablas
He/She/It speaks → Él/Ella habla
We speak    →  Nosotros hablamos
You all speak → Vosotros habláis (Spain) / Ustedes hablan (Latin America)
They speak  →  Ellos/Ellas hablan

EXAMPLES (Real-world):
1. "Yo hablo español" (I speak Spanish)
2. "Mi hermana habla inglés" (My sister speaks English)
3. "Nosotros hablamos en la clase" (We speak in class)
4. "¿Tú hablas francés?" (Do you speak French?)

COMMON EXCEPTIONS:
None for regular -AR verbs (but note that there ARE irregular verbs like ser, estar, ir)

PRACTICE EXERCISES:

Exercise 1: Conjugate the verb "estudiar" (to study)
1. Yo _____ (study) español
2. Tú _____ (study) mucho
3. Mi profesor _____ (studies) todos los días
4. Nosotros _____ (study) en la biblioteca

Exercise 2: Transformation - Turn statements into questions
Example: "Yo hablo español" → "¿Tú hablas español?"
1. "Mi hermana trabaja en una tienda" → "¿Tú _____?"
2. "Nosotros bailamos en la fiesta" → "¿Ustedes _____?"

Exercise 3: Fill in the correct form:
1. Yo _____ (hablar) con mis amigos
2. Ellas _____ (cantar) en el coro
3. Nosotros _____ (comprar) pan en la panadería

ANSWER KEY:
Exercise 1: 1. estudio, 2. estudias, 3. estudia, 4. estudiamos
Exercise 2: 1. trabajas, 2. bailan
Exercise 3: 1. hablo, 2. cantan, 3. compramos
```

**Design Rationale**:
- Show pattern first (inductive learning)
- Provide real examples before abstraction
- Keep practice exercises highly controlled (learner should be able to do them)
- Note exceptions immediately if they exist

### Cultural: Food Culture at A1 Level

**Activity**: "Breakfast Around the World" Cultural Guide

**Design**:

```
BREAKFAST AROUND THE WORLD

Objective: Learn about typical breakfasts in target culture and compare to your own

SPAIN - El Desayuno:
What: Coffee with a pastry or toast
Typical items:
- Café con tostadas (Coffee with toast)
- Café con churros (Coffee with fried pastries)
- Café con un bocadillo (Coffee with a small sandwich)

Time: 7–9 AM (quick!)
Cultural note: Breakfast in Spain is much smaller than in English-speaking countries.
The big meal is lunch (2–3 PM). If you're a tourist expecting a "full breakfast,"
you'll be surprised!

Vocabulary:
- café (KAH-feh): coffee
- tostadas (TOHS-tah-dahs): toast
- churro (CHOO-roh): fried pastry (singular)
- bocadillo (boh-cah-DHEE-yoh): small sandwich

FRANCE - Le Petit Déjeuner:
What: Coffee with bread and jam (or a pastry)
Typical items:
- Café avec pain et confiture (Coffee with bread and jam)
- Croissant (flaky pastry)
- Chocolat chaud (Hot chocolate)

Time: 7–8 AM
Cultural note: French breakfast is also light. Croissants and pastries are for
special occasions, not daily. Pain (bread) with butter and jam is most common.

Vocabulary:
- pain (pahn): bread
- confiture (con-fee-TUR): jam
- croissant (KRAH-sahn): crescent-shaped pastry
- chocolat chaud (shoh-koh-LAH SHOH): hot chocolate

GERMANY - Das Frühstück:
What: Bread, cheese, cold cuts, soft-boiled eggs, coffee
Typical items:
- Brötchen mit Butter und Marmelade (Roll with butter and jam)
- Wurst (Cold cuts/sausage)
- Käse (Cheese)
- Ei (Egg)

Time: 6–8 AM
Cultural note: German breakfast is heartier than French or Spanish. Bread rolls
are often fresh from the bakery. This breakfast is meant to keep you full until lunch.

Vocabulary:
- Brötchen (BRŒ-tchen): roll/small bread
- Wurst (VOORST): sausage/cold cuts
- Käse (KÄ-zer): cheese
- Ei (AY): egg

COMPARISON QUESTIONS:
1. Which breakfast is most similar to yours?
2. What's the difference between Spanish and German breakfasts?
3. In your country, do you eat breakfast quickly or slowly?
4. What's your favorite breakfast food? How would you order it in [target language]?

VOCABULARY SUMMARY (English → Target Language):
Breakfast - Desayuno / Petit déjeuner / Frühstück
Bread - Pan / Pain / Brot
Coffee - Café / Café / Kaffee
Egg - Huevo / Œuf / Ei
Cheese - Queso / Fromage / Käse
```

**Why This Works at A1**:
- Real, familiar context (breakfast)
- Concrete, visual vocabulary
- Comparison deepens cultural awareness
- Sets foundation for later, deeper cultural learning

## A2 Level (Elementary) Immersion Activities

### Reading: Simple Narrative with Past Events

**Activity**: "My Favorite Weekend" Personal Essay

**Objective**:
- Read past-tense narratives with some structural complexity
- Build confidence with temporal connectors (entonces, después, luego)
- Extract main ideas and supporting details

**Design**:

```
TEXT (150–180 words, Spanish example):

Mi fin de semana favorito
(My Favorite Weekend)

El fin de semana pasado fue especial. Me desperté a las diez de la mañana
y desayuné con mi familia. Después, mis amigos y yo fuimos al parque
para jugar al fútbol.

Hacía buen tiempo. El sol brillaba y no hacía demasiado calor. Jugamos
durante dos horas. Mi equipo ganó, así que todos estábamos muy felices.

Más tarde, fuimos a una pequeña cafetería. Pedimos bebidas y hablamos
de nuestros planes. Mi amigo Carlos me contó que quería viajar a Italia
el próximo verano.

Por la noche, volví a casa y miré mi serie favorita en la televisión.
Fue un día perfecto.

VOCABULARY GLOSSARY:
- fin de semana (FIN deh seh-MAH-nah): weekend
- fue (FOO-eh): was
- desayunar: to have breakfast
- despertar: to wake up
- después (des-PWES): after, afterward
- parque (PAR-keh): park
- fútbol (FOOT-bohl): football/soccer
- hacía buen tiempo (ah-SEE-ah bwen tee-EM-poh): the weather was good
- brillar: to shine
- jugar: to play
- ganar: to win
- feliz (feh-LEES): happy
- bebida (beh-BEE-dah): drink
- contar: to tell/to count
- viajar: to travel
- siguiente (see-gee-EN-teh): next
- televisión (teh-leh-vee-see-OHN): television

COMPREHENSION QUESTIONS:
1. ¿A qué hora se despertó? (Factual) [Answer: A las diez]
2. ¿Qué actividad hizo por la mañana? (Factual) [Answer: Desayunó y desayunó]
3. ¿Qué jugó con sus amigos? (Factual) [Answer: Fútbol]
4. ¿Qué equipo ganó? (Factual) [Answer: Su equipo]
5. ¿Adónde fueron después del parque? (Factual) [Answer: A una cafetería]
6. ¿Por qué fue un día perfecto para el narrador? (Inferential)
   [Possible answer: Porque su equipo ganó, pasó tiempo con amigos, y disfrutó]

GRAMMAR FOCUS:
Highlight past tense formations in the text:
- Regular past: "desayuné," "desperté," "jugamos"
- Irregular past: "fue," "fuimos," "vieron"
- Temporal connectors: "después," "más tarde," "el próximo"

CULTURAL NOTE:
In Spain and most of Latin America, fútbol (football/soccer) is THE most popular sport.
Weekend activities often revolve around sports, family meals, and time with friends.
The casual time spent in cafés talking is a cultural characteristic worth noting.

DISCUSSION QUESTIONS:
1. ¿Cuál fue tu fin de semana favorito? Describe your favorite weekend.
2. ¿Qué deportes te gustan? What sports do you like?
3. ¿Prefieres estar con amigos o con familia en el fin de semana?
```

### Conversation: Phone Call and Making Plans

**Activity**: "Calling a Friend to Plan Weekend" Scenario

**Design**:

```
SCENARIO: Planning Weekend Activities by Phone

Situation:
You call your friend to ask what they're doing this weekend. You want to invite
them to do something together.

Key Vocabulary:
- ¿Qué haces? (What are you doing?)
- quiero hacer (I want to do)
- ¿Quieres...? (Do you want to...?)
- película (moh-VEE-lah): movie
- parque (park)
- piscina (pee-THEE-nah): swimming pool
- jugar (to play)
- ir (to go)
- sábado (SAH-bah-doh): Saturday
- domingo (doh-MEEN-goh): Sunday
- hora (OH-rah): time, hour
- lugar (loo-GAR): place

Useful Expressions:

GREETING BY PHONE:
- "¿Hola, cómo estás?" (Hello, how are you?)
- "Soy [name], ¿cómo estás?" (It's [name], how are you?)

ASKING ABOUT PLANS:
- "¿Qué haces el sábado?" (What are you doing Saturday?)
- "¿Estás ocupado este fin de semana?" (Are you busy this weekend?)
- "¿Qué planes tienes?" (What plans do you have?)

MAKING SUGGESTIONS:
- "¿Quieres ir al cine?" (Do you want to go to the movies?)
- "Podríamos jugar al fútbol" (We could play soccer)
- "¿Te gustaría ir al parque?" (Would you like to go to the park?)

AGREEING/DISAGREEING:
- "¡Buena idea!" (Good idea!)
- "Sí, me encanta" (Yes, I love it)
- "No, no puedo" (No, I can't)
- "Tengo otro plan" (I have another plan)

MAKING SPECIFIC ARRANGEMENTS:
- "¿A qué hora?" (What time?)
- "¿Dónde nos encontramos?" (Where shall we meet?)
- "¿Te parece bien a las tres?" (Does 3 o'clock seem good to you?)

CLOSING:
- "Hasta el sábado" (See you Saturday)
- "¡Que divertido!" (How fun!)

Cultural Context:
- In many target cultures, phone calls for social planning are still common (not just texting)
- Young people may use "tú" (informal you) with friends; use formal "usted" only with adults/strangers
- Answering a friend's call with their name is polite: "¿Hola, Carlos?" (Is that you, Carlos?)

ROLE A (You - The Caller):
Goal: Call a friend, find out their weekend plans, suggest doing something together
Opening: "¡Hola! Soy [your name]. ¿Qué haces el fin de semana?"
Key points:
- Ask what they're doing
- Suggest an activity you like
- Agree on time and place
Challenge: They're not interested in your first suggestion; offer an alternative

ROLE B (Friend Receiving Call):
Goal: Answer the phone, respond to their plans, agree on an activity
Opening: "¿Hola?"
Key points:
- Identify yourself
- Tell them what you were planning
- React to their suggestion
- Suggest a specific time/place
Challenge: You're uncertain about one activity; ask questions before agreeing

CONVERSATION FLOW SUGGESTION:
1. Friend answers: "¿Hola?"
2. You identify yourself and greet: "¡Hola! Soy [name]. ¿Qué tal?"
3. You ask about their weekend: "¿Qué haces el sábado?"
4. Friend tells their plan (or says they're free)
5. You suggest an activity: "¿Quieres ir al cine?"
6. Friend reacts (maybe asks more questions or agrees)
7. You arrange time: "¿A las tres?"
8. Friend agrees and suggests meeting place
9. Both say goodbye: "Hasta el sábado. ¡Adiós!"

Common Mistakes to Avoid:
- Don't say "¿Qué estás haciendo?" (What ARE you doing RIGHT NOW?) when you mean future plans; use "¿Qué haces?"
- Don't forget the question mark at the beginning in Spanish: "¿Qué haces?"
- Don't use "usted" with friends; use "tú"
- Don't hang up without confirming time and place

FOLLOW-UP DISCUSSION:
1. What activities do you like to do with friends?
2. How would you suggest meeting for dinner instead of movies?
3. What would you say if your friend cancels?
4. Practice: Call someone (or role-play) and plan an actual activity
```

## B1 Level (Intermediate) Immersion Activities

### Reading: Magazine Article or Short News Story

**Activity**: "Technology in Everyday Life" Article

**Objective**:
- Comprehend longer, more complex texts (300–400 words)
- Handle varied vocabulary with less direct glossing
- Extract main ideas and support details
- Recognize text organization and author purpose

**Design**:

```
TITLE: La tecnología en la vida cotidiana
(Technology in Everyday Life)

ARTICLE (approximately 350 words):

[Complete article in target language - example structure:]

Opening: State the topic and its relevance
Body paragraph 1: How technology affects work
Body paragraph 2: How technology affects social life
Body paragraph 3: Challenges of technology
Conclusion: Balance and future

VOCABULARY GLOSSARY (for approximately 20 words):
Provide definitions only for words genuinely above B1 level.
Assume learner knows most common words.

Examples:
- cotidiano/a (koh-tee-AH-noh): daily, everyday
- vínculo (VEEN-koo-loh): bond, connection
- desconexión (des-koh-nex-ee-OHN): disconnection
- pantalla (pahn-TAH-yah): screen
[etc.]

COMPREHENSION QUESTIONS:

Literal (fact-finding):
1. Según el artículo, ¿cuáles son dos usos de la tecnología mencionados?
   (According to the article, what are two uses of technology mentioned?)

2. ¿Qué desafío de la tecnología menciona el autor?
   (What challenge of technology does the author mention?)

Inferential (reading between the lines):
3. ¿Cuál es la actitud del autor hacia la tecnología—positiva, negativa, o equilibrada?
   (What is the author's attitude toward technology—positive, negative, or balanced?)

4. ¿Por qué crees que el autor menciona "desconexión" en el artículo?
   (Why do you think the author mentions "disconnection"?)

Evaluative (your opinion):
5. ¿Estás de acuerdo con las opiniones del autor? ¿Por qué?
   (Do you agree with the author's opinions? Why?)

GRAMMAR FOCUS:
Highlight complex sentences using subordination (porque, aunque, sin embargo):
"Aunque la tecnología ha mejorado nuestras vidas, debemos recordar..."
(Although technology has improved our lives, we must remember...)

CULTURAL NOTE:
In target culture, discuss attitudes toward technology, work-life balance,
and generational differences in technology adoption.

DISCUSSION QUESTIONS:
1. ¿Cuántas horas al día usas la tecnología?
   (How many hours a day do you use technology?)

2. ¿Cuál es tu app o sitio web favorito? ¿Por qué?
   (What's your favorite app or website? Why?)

3. ¿Crees que la tecnología nos acerca o nos aleja?
   (Do you think technology brings us together or pushes us apart?)

4. ¿Qué tecnología te gustaría que existiera?
   (What technology would you like to exist?)
```

### Conversation: Expressing Opinions and Disagreement

**Activity**: "Discussing Favorite Movies or TV Shows"

**Design**:

```
SCENARIO: Chatting with a New Friend About Entertainment Preferences

Situation:
You meet someone at a social event. You discover you both watch TV or go to movies.
You discuss your favorite shows, react to their preferences, and debate the merits
of different programs.

Key Vocabulary:
- telenovela (teh-leh-noh-VEH-lah): soap opera
- serie (seh-REE-eh): TV series/show
- película de acción (peh-LEE-koo-lah deh ahk-see-OHN): action movie
- comedia (koh-MEH-dee-ah): comedy
- drama (DRAH-mah): drama
- entretenida/o (en-treh-teh-NEE-dah): entertaining
- aburrida/o (ah-boo-RREE-dah): boring
- emocionante (eh-moh-see-oh-NAHN-teh): exciting
- me encanta (I love it)
- me parece (it seems to me)

Useful Expressions:

ASKING ABOUT PREFERENCES:
- "¿Qué tipo de películas te gustan?" (What kind of movies do you like?)
- "¿Ves series? ¿Cuál es tu favorita?" (Do you watch TV series? What's your favorite?)
- "¿Qué tal esa serie que salió el mes pasado?" (What about that show that came out last month?)

EXPRESSING OPINIONS:
- "Creo que es una película excelente" (I think it's an excellent movie)
- "En mi opinión, es muy entretenida" (In my opinion, it's very entertaining)
- "Me parece que es demasiado larga" (It seems to me it's too long)

AGREEING:
- "Totalmente de acuerdo" (Totally agree)
- "Tienes razón" (You're right)
- "Exacto" (Exactly)

DISAGREEING POLITELY:
- "No estoy de acuerdo" (I don't agree)
- "Puede ser, pero..." (Maybe, but...)
- "Entiendo tu punto, sin embargo..." (I understand your point, however...)
- "Yo veo las cosas diferente" (I see things differently)

EXPRESSING UNCERTAINTY:
- "No sé qué pensar" (I don't know what to think)
- "Nunca la he visto" (I've never seen it)
- "No he oído de esa" (I've never heard of that one)

ASKING FOR MORE INFO:
- "¿De qué trata?" (What's it about?)
- "¿Es buena?" (Is it good?)
- "¿Cuántas temporadas tiene?" (How many seasons does it have?)

Cultural Context:
- In many target cultures, watching TV as a family is common; sharing recommendations is
  a way to bond
- In Spain, telenovelas are often from Latin America and are quite popular
- German cinema and Scandinavian series have strong reputations; discuss these
- In target culture, there may be distinct preferences by region or generation

ROLE A (You):
Goal: Engage in conversation about entertainment, share your opinion,
respond to their suggestions
Opening: "¿Ves mucha televisión?" or "¿Qué tipo de películas te gustan?"
Key points:
- Ask about their preferences
- Share your own favorites
- Defend your opinion if they disagree
- Ask about specific shows/movies you've heard of
Challenge: They like something you dislike; find common ground

ROLE B (Friend):
Goal: Discuss their entertainment preferences, be open to different opinions,
make recommendations
Opening: Answer their question enthusiastically
Key points:
- Share your favorite show/movie
- Explain why you like it
- React to their opinion
- Suggest something they might like
Challenge: You haven't seen what they're recommending; ask thoughtful questions

CONVERSATION FLOW:
1. You ask: "¿Ves mucha televisión?"
2. Friend responds and asks what you watch
3. You describe your favorite show/movie
4. Friend gives their opinion (agrees or disagrees)
5. You react and ask about THEIR favorite
6. Friend describes theirs
7. You might agree or respectfully disagree
8. One of you makes a recommendation
9. You discuss whether you'll watch it
10. Closing: "Deberíamos verla juntos" (We should watch it together)

Common Mistakes to Avoid:
- Don't confuse "me gusta" (I like) with "me encanta" (I love)—use the stronger
  form for things you really enjoy
- Don't use "yo creo que" (I believe) for factual statements; use "pienso que"
  or "creo que" more generally
- Don't forget to use "aunque" (although) when you disagree but respect their view
- Avoid: "No estás de acuerdo" (wrong); say "No estoy de acuerdo" or "Estoy en desacuerdo"

FOLLOW-UP DISCUSSION:
1. How do people in target culture choose what to watch?
2. What's the most popular TV show or movie right now in target culture?
3. How would you convince a friend to watch something you love?
```

### Grammar Reference: Subjunctive Mood (Basic)

**Activity**: "When and How to Express Doubt, Desire, and Emotion"

**Design**:

```
GRAMMAR FOCUS: Introduction to the Subjunctive Mood

PATTERN EXPLANATION (Inductive):

In Spanish, there are TWO main moods: Indicative (statements of fact)
and Subjunctive (expressions of doubt, desire, emotion, or hypothetical situations).

Compare:
INDICATIVE (statement of fact): "Creo que es verdad" (I believe it's true)
SUBJUNCTIVE (doubt): "No creo que sea verdad" (I don't believe it's true)

INDICATIVE (statement): "Ella viene mañana" (She's coming tomorrow)
SUBJUNCTIVE (desire): "Espero que venga mañana" (I hope [that] she comes tomorrow)

KEY TRIGGERS FOR SUBJUNCTIVE:

1. DOUBT / DENIAL:
   - No creo que... (I don't believe that...)
   - Dudo que... (I doubt that...)
   - No es verdad que... (It's not true that...)

2. DESIRE / REQUEST:
   - Quiero que... (I want [that]...)
   - Te pido que... (I ask you [that]...)
   - Espero que... (I hope [that]...)

3. EMOTION:
   - Me alegra que... (I'm happy [that]...)
   - Tengo miedo que... (I'm afraid [that]...)
   - Lamento que... (I regret [that]...)

4. HYPOTHETICAL / CONDITION:
   - Si fuera rico... (If I were rich...)
   - Como si... (As if...)

CLEAR EXAMPLES (Present Subjunctive):

DOUBT:
- "No creo que él sepa la respuesta"
  (I don't think he knows the answer)
  Compare to: "Creo que él sabe la respuesta" (indicative—I think he knows)

- "Dudo que vengan a la fiesta"
  (I doubt they'll come to the party)
  Compare to: "Creo que vienen a la fiesta" (indicative—I think they're coming)

DESIRE:
- "Quiero que estudies más"
  (I want you to study more)
  Compare to: "Estudias mucho" (indicative—you study a lot)

- "Espero que tengas suerte en la entrevista"
  (I hope you have luck in the interview)

EMOTION:
- "Me alegra que estés feliz"
  (I'm happy that you're happy)

- "Tengo miedo de que se pierda"
  (I'm afraid he'll get lost / I'm afraid that he gets lost)

COMMON IRREGULAR SUBJUNCTIVES:
- ser → sea, seas, seamos, seáis, sean
- estar → esté, estés, etc.
- ir → vaya, vayas, etc.
- haber → haya, hayas, etc.

PRACTICE EXERCISES:

Exercise 1: Identify Indicative vs. Subjunctive
Which sentence uses the subjunctive? (Answer: all with subjunctive triggers)
1. a) "Sé que comes pizza" (I know you eat pizza)
   b) "Espero que comas pizza" (I hope you eat pizza) ✓

2. a) "Ella viene mañana" (She's coming tomorrow)
   b) "Dudo que venga mañana" (I doubt she's coming tomorrow) ✓

Exercise 2: Fill in with Present Subjunctive
1. No creo que él ________ (venir) a la clase mañana
2. Quiero que tú ________ (hablar) español en la clase
3. Me alegra que ________ (estar) aquí
4. Dudo que ________ (haber) suficiente tiempo

Exercise 3: Transformation - Change from Indicative to Subjunctive
Example: "Sé que ella es inteligente" → "Espero que sea inteligente"

1. "Creo que es divertido" → "Espero que ________"
2. "Ella viene mañana" → "Quiero que ________"
3. "Tienes razón" → "No creo que ________"

ANSWER KEY:
Exercise 2: 1. venga, 2. hables, 3. estés (singular) or estemos (plural), 4. haya
Exercise 3: 1. sea divertido, 2. venga mañana, 3. tengas razón

WHEN NOT TO USE SUBJUNCTIVE:
Remember: Use indicative in these cases (very common mistake):
- After "creo que" (I think that) in POSITIVE statements
  ✓ "Creo que es verdad" (I think it's true)
  ✗ "Creo que sea verdad" (wrong—only use subjunctive if you DON'T believe)

- After "cuando" (when), "porque" (because), "si" (if—meaning "when/in the future")
  ✓ "Cuando llegues, llámame" (When you arrive, call me)
  ✗ "Cuando llegas" only in present habitual (When you arrive, you...) [indicative is correct here]

Why This Matters:
The subjunctive is a huge leap in Spanish proficiency. It forces you to think about
the reality/certainty of what you're expressing. This is one of the most important
grammar concepts in Spanish intermediate learning.
```

### Cultural Guide: Workplace Culture and Business Etiquette

**Activity**: "Working and Doing Business in [Target Culture]"

**Design**:

```
CULTURAL GUIDE: Workplace Etiquette in Spain

OBJECTIVE:
Understand workplace norms, communication styles, and business culture in the target culture
so you can interact professionally in that context.

SECTION 1: GENERAL WORKPLACE CULTURE

Spanish Workplace Characteristics:
- Hierarchical structure (boss is boss; don't expect informal relationships across ranks)
- Relationship-focused (building personal connection is important before business)
- Long lunch break is sacred (1.5–2 hours, often at home or with colleagues)
- Siesta culture (some businesses still close 2–4 PM in smaller towns)
- Work hours: typically 8 AM–2 PM, then 4 PM–7 PM (in areas without siesta)
- Summer: Many Spanish people take 2–3 weeks vacation in August; plan accordingly

Compare to Your Culture:
- How long is a typical lunch break in your country?
- Do people often go home for lunch?
- What are typical work hours?

SECTION 2: GREETINGS AND TITLES

Formal Greeting (first meeting or with supervisors):
- "Buenos días / Buenas tardes, Sr./Sra. [Last name]"
  (Good morning / afternoon, Mr./Ms. [Last name])
- Handshake is standard
- Wait to use first name until invited

Informal Greeting (colleagues, same level):
- "¡Hola! ¿Qué tal?" (Hi! How are you?)
- Two-cheek kiss (beso) is common in Spain—once on each cheek
- In Latin America, handshake is more common; use beso only if invited

Key Titles:
- Señor / Señora (Mr. / Mrs.)
- Don / Doña (respectful form, often used for older people or those in positions of respect)
- Jefe (boss/chief)
- Director/a (Director)
- Gerente (Manager)

SECTION 3: BUSINESS COMMUNICATION

Email Culture:
- Formal greeting: "Estimado/a [Name]," (Dear [Name])
- Formal closing: "Atentamente" or "Saludos cordiales" (Sincerely, Best regards)
- Emails are more formal than in English-speaking cultures
- Response time: Usually within 24 hours expected

Phone Calls:
- Identify yourself: "Hola, buenos días. Soy [Your name] from [Company]. ¿Está [Person]?"
- Be respectful of lunch hours; avoid calling 1–4 PM if possible
- Spanish people tend to be direct on the phone; don't interpret this as rude

Meetings:
- Meetings may start 5–10 minutes late (time is more flexible)
- Agenda is often loose; conversation can flow in different directions
- Let the boss speak first
- Disagreement is okay if stated respectfully: "Entiendo tu punto, sin embargo..."

SECTION 4: KEY BUSINESS IDIOMS AND EXPRESSIONS

- "Ponerse de acuerdo" (Reach an agreement)
- "Tomar una decisión" (Make a decision)
- "Llevar a cabo" (Carry out)
- "Estar al tanto" (Be aware/updated)
- "Darse cuenta de" (Realize)
- "De acuerdo" (Agreed)
- "¿De verdad?" (Really? / Is that so?)
- "Siendo sincero/a" (To be honest)

SECTION 5: FOOD AND BUSINESS MEALS

Business Lunch Culture:
- Lunch is the main meal and a time for relationship-building
- Typically 1–2 hours
- Alcohol (wine or beer) may be served; it's acceptable to have a small glass
- Small talk before discussing business is normal
- In Spain, you might say "¿Qué tal es [Restaurant]?" to get recommendations

Vocabulary:
- Almuerzo (lunch—the main meal)
- Comida (lunch or meal in general)
- Cena (dinner—lighter than lunch)
- Mesero / Camarero (waiter)
- La cuenta (the bill)
- Propina (tip)

SECTION 6: BUSINESS DRESS AND APPEARANCE

Spain:
- Business formal: Suit and tie for men; suit or dress for women
- Business casual: No jeans; clean, neat clothes
- In summer, business casual may shift slightly (no jacket, short sleeves acceptable)
- Appearance is noticed; looking put-together matters

Different cultures vary; check your specific business context.

SECTION 7: IMPORTANT CULTURAL DIFFERENCES

Spanish Work-Life Balance:
- Spanish culture prioritizes personal life and family time
- It's acceptable to mention family commitments, vacation plans, hobbies
- Small talk about personal life is normal and relationship-building

Time Management:
- More flexible than English-speaking cultures
- Deadlines are guidelines; extensions are negotiable if requested
- Punctuality for meetings is expected but 5–10 minutes late is "Spanish time"

Relationships:
- Building trust and personal relationship comes BEFORE major business
- Multiple meetings might be needed before a decision
- Socializing outside work strengthens business relationships

SECTION 8: MEDIA AND RESOURCE RECOMMENDATIONS

Spanish Business Spanish Learning:
- Podcasts: "News in Slow Spanish" (intermediate content on current business topics)
- Website: businessspanish.com (specific business vocabulary)
- Book: "Doing Business in [Country]" guides
- Movies about business: Documentaries about famous Spanish companies (INDITEX, Telefónica, etc.)

DISCUSSION QUESTIONS:

1. ¿Cuál es la diferencia principal entre la cultura de trabajo de tu país y la de [target culture]?
   (What's the main difference between the work culture of your country and [target culture]?)

2. ¿Cómo saludarías a tu jefe? ¿Y a un colega de tu mismo nivel?
   (How would you greet your boss? And a colleague at your same level?)

3. ¿Qué aspecto de la cultura de trabajo hispana te sorprende más?
   (What aspect of Hispanic work culture surprises you most?)

4. ¿Cómo es una comida de negocios en tu país?
   (What's a business meal like in your country?)

5. Role-play: Attend a business lunch and practice small talk in the target language
```

## B2–C1 Level Activities (Advanced)

### Reading: Literary Excerpt or Sophisticated Article

**Activity**: Short story excerpt or essay on a complex social issue

**Design Elements**:
- 800+ words
- Rich vocabulary including synonyms with subtle differences
- Complex syntax and narrative techniques
- Minimal glossing; focus on truly challenging words
- Comprehension questions spanning recall, analysis, and critical evaluation
- Links to cultural/literary context
- Essay discussion prompts

**Activity**: "Discussing Technology's Impact on Society"

**Design Elements**:
- Requires expressing nuanced positions
- Presenting counterarguments respectfully
- Using subjunctive for conditional statements
- Idioms and sophisticated vocabulary
- Building and defending complex arguments
- Acknowledging multiple perspectives

## Universal Principles Across All Levels

### 1. Comprehensibility
Every material should follow the "i+1" principle: slightly above current level, with support making it comprehensible.

### 2. Authenticity-Feeling
Avoid obviously constructed textbook content. Use realistically situated contexts.

### 3. Multiple Exposures
Vocabulary and structures should appear across different material types and contexts.

### 4. Cultural Embedding
Language is inseparable from culture. Every material should include cultural context.

### 5. Relevance
Content should connect to learner's goals, interests, and established learning trajectory.

### 6. Progression
Move systematically from guided to independent practice, from concrete to abstract.

### 7. Integration
Materials should reinforce topics from recent sessions and support vocabulary from spaced repetition practice.
