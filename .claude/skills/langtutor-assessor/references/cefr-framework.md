# CEFR Framework: Complete Can-Do Descriptors

This document provides detailed, actionable descriptors for each CEFR level (A1-C2) across all six language skills. Use this to assess learners, explain levels clearly, and set appropriate milestones.

---

## A1 - Elementary (Beginner)

**Overall Descriptor**: Can recognize and use very basic expressions and words needed to handle simple, concrete tasks. Communication depends on repetition and a willingness to cooperate.

### Reading A1
- [ ] Can read very simple, short texts on familiar subjects
- [ ] Can understand isolated words and simple, predictable phrases
- [ ] Can pick out basic information from notices (menus, advertisements, hotel descriptions)
- [ ] Understands personal messages, postcards, or very short emails on familiar topics
- [ ] Can follow simple written instructions (e.g., recipes with pictures)

### Writing A1
- [ ] Can write simple, short messages (greeting cards, postcards)
- [ ] Can fill in basic forms with personal information (name, address, email)
- [ ] Can write simple sentences about themselves or their family
- [ ] Can copy or write familiar words and very basic phrases
- [ ] Cannot sustain coherent writing beyond single sentences

### Listening A1
- [ ] Can recognize familiar words and very basic phrases concerning themselves, their family, shopping, local geography
- [ ] Can catch the main words in utterances spoken slowly and clearly
- [ ] Understands greetings, farewells, and basic courtesy phrases
- [ ] Can understand common commands and simple questions if spoken slowly
- [ ] Relies heavily on visual support and context clues

### Speaking A1
- [ ] Can use simple greetings and say goodbye appropriately
- [ ] Can introduce themselves and others in very basic terms
- [ ] Can answer simple, predictable questions about personal information
- [ ] Can produce isolated words and short, memorized phrases
- [ ] Cannot engage in spontaneous conversation

### Vocabulary A1
- [ ] Vocabulary range: 500-1,000 words
- [ ] Can use basic words for: numbers (0-100), colors, days/months, family, animals, food, clothing, rooms/furniture, common verbs (to be, to have, to go)
- [ ] Vocabulary is recognition-based; active use is limited to memorized chunks
- [ ] Can name common objects in the immediate environment

### Grammar A1
- [ ] Uses present tense with high frequency (to be, to have)
- [ ] Can use basic negation (not, no)
- [ ] Uses simple declarative sentences
- [ ] Limited use of question formation; relies on rising intonation
- [ ] Can use infinitives in basic contexts ("I want to...")
- [ ] Little to no use of complex structures

---

## A2 - Elementary (Post-Beginner)

**Overall Descriptor**: Can handle simple, routine tasks requiring direct exchange of information on familiar topics. Can describe themselves, their family, daily routines, education, and present or past activities in simple terms.

### Reading A2
- [ ] Can read short, personal texts on familiar subjects (emails from friends, simple blog posts)
- [ ] Can understand the main points in short, simple texts about familiar topics
- [ ] Can find specific information in simple, predictable text (timetables, advertisements, simple news articles)
- [ ] Can understand simple written instructions and follow multi-step directions
- [ ] Can read short narratives about daily events in simple past tense

### Writing A2
- [ ] Can write short, simple personal messages and emails
- [ ] Can describe themselves, their family, and daily routines in connected sentences
- [ ] Can write about past events using simple past tense
- [ ] Can fill in detailed forms (job applications, hotel reservations)
- [ ] Can write a simple personal description (for dating profile, social media bio)
- [ ] Writing is mostly grammatically correct but with occasional errors

### Listening A2
- [ ] Can understand the main words in conversations about familiar topics (family, hobbies, work)
- [ ] Can recognize the general topic and key details in simple broadcasts (weather, basic news)
- [ ] Can understand simple directions and instructions
- [ ] Can follow simple narratives about daily activities
- [ ] Understands better when speech is clear, slower than native pace

### Speaking A2
- [ ] Can introduce themselves and others, state occupation, education, family situation
- [ ] Can ask and answer simple questions about personal information and daily routines
- [ ] Can describe their family, education, work, and interests in simple sentences
- [ ] Can give a simple, connected account of past events ("I went to the store. I bought bread.")
- [ ] Can handle very simple exchanges in routine contexts (ordering food, basic hotel check-in)
- [ ] Speech is hesitant; requires time to think and formulate responses

### Vocabulary A2
- [ ] Vocabulary range: 1,000-1,500 words
- [ ] Can use words for: extended family relations, basic professions, common workplace terms, food/restaurants, simple travel vocabulary, household items, weather, common hobbies
- [ ] Beginning to move from recognition to active production
- [ ] Can describe simple daily routines and common activities

### Grammar A2
- [ ] Can use present and simple past tenses reliably
- [ ] Can form basic questions and negations independently
- [ ] Can use common modal verbs (can, should, want to)
- [ ] Beginning use of "going to" for future
- [ ] Can use simple conjunctions (and, but, because)
- [ ] Occasional errors in agreement and tense consistency

---

## B1 - Intermediate

**Overall Descriptor**: Can produce simple, connected text on familiar topics. Can describe experiences, events, dreams, hopes, and ambitions. Can give reasons and explanations for opinions and plans. Handles the language of routine situations and can handle unexpected situations.

### Reading B1
- [ ] Can read straightforward texts on familiar topics within their field of interest
- [ ] Can understand the main ideas in texts (news articles, blog posts, reviews)
- [ ] Can find and extract specific information from articles, advertisements, instruction manuals
- [ ] Can follow the plot of a simple narrative or short story
- [ ] Can understand personal letters describing events and feelings
- [ ] Can handle texts with some unfamiliar words if context aids understanding

### Writing B1
- [ ] Can write connected texts on familiar topics (personal experiences, opinions about film/book)
- [ ] Can write an email or letter describing an event, expressing feelings and offering explanations
- [ ] Can write a simple description of a place or person
- [ ] Can summarize the main points of an article or story
- [ ] Writing is generally well-organized with clear sequencing
- [ ] Generally grammatically correct, though occasional errors in complex structures

### Listening B1
- [ ] Can understand the main points in a speech or presentation on a familiar topic
- [ ] Can understand most of a radio program or podcast on familiar subjects
- [ ] Can catch the main points in a film or video with standard accent
- [ ] Can follow a conversation about familiar topics between native speakers if spoken clearly
- [ ] Can understand the essence of news bulletins and simple reports
- [ ] May miss some details if speech is rapid or uses unfamiliar accents

### Speaking B1
- [ ] Can initiate, maintain, and close conversations on familiar topics
- [ ] Can give opinions on social and political issues and explain their viewpoint with reasons
- [ ] Can describe experiences, events, and dreams; express hopes and ambitions
- [ ] Can give detailed personal information
- [ ] Can handle most situations encountered while traveling
- [ ] Can participate in small group discussions with effort
- [ ] Speech is mostly fluent but occasionally pauses to find words

### Vocabulary B1
- [ ] Vocabulary range: 1,500-2,500 words
- [ ] Can discuss: current events, social issues, entertainment, travel, personal relationships, work/career development
- [ ] Can understand and use common idiomatic expressions
- [ ] Can express opinions with supporting vocabulary
- [ ] Beginning awareness of register differences (formal vs. informal)

### Grammar B1
- [ ] Can use past, present, and future tenses with general accuracy
- [ ] Can use a variety of modal verbs (must, might, could, should, would)
- [ ] Can form complex sentences with subordinate clauses (because, although, if)
- [ ] Can use relative clauses
- [ ] Generally accurate agreement and tense sequencing, though some errors remain
- [ ] May struggle with subjunctive mood or conditional structures

---

## B2 - Upper-Intermediate (Vantage)

**Overall Descriptor**: Can produce clear, well-organized, detailed text on complex subjects and can sustain arguments, underlining the relevant points. Can interact with native speakers at length without strain for either party.

### Reading B2
- [ ] Can read a wide range of texts on topics within their interests and specialization
- [ ] Can understand detailed texts, reports, analyses, and opinion pieces
- [ ] Can follow complex arguments even on unfamiliar topics
- [ ] Can understand implicit meaning, tone, and author attitude
- [ ] Can quickly identify key information in longer texts
- [ ] Can extract detailed information from dense texts

### Writing B2
- [ ] Can write clear, well-structured essays or reports on complex topics
- [ ] Can express and justify opinions, analyze different perspectives
- [ ] Can write detailed descriptions of complex processes or situations
- [ ] Can write formal or informal letters with appropriate register
- [ ] Can provide detailed accounts of experiences with good structure and flow
- [ ] Writing is mostly error-free; errors do not impede communication

### Listening B2
- [ ] Can understand extended speech on abstract and concrete topics
- [ ] Can follow complex arguments in discussions or debates
- [ ] Can understand documentaries, news analysis, and technical presentations
- [ ] Can recognize speaker attitude, tone, and implied meaning
- [ ] Can understand most films and programs with standard accents
- [ ] Can follow fast, native-speed speech with occasional difficulty

### Speaking B2
- [ ] Can participate actively in discussions on a wide range of topics
- [ ] Can express and justify complex opinions; can argue both sides
- [ ] Can give detailed presentations on topics of interest
- [ ] Can handle spontaneous, unprepared speech reasonably fluently
- [ ] Can discuss abstract concepts (politics, philosophy, psychology)
- [ ] Can correct themselves and repair communication breakdowns
- [ ] Speaks with relatively few pauses; generally fluent with occasional hesitation

### Vocabulary B2
- [ ] Vocabulary range: 2,500-3,500 words
- [ ] Can discuss: abstract topics, social and political issues, literature, history, science basics
- [ ] Good command of idiomatic expressions and colloquialisms
- [ ] Can understand and use words with nuanced meanings
- [ ] Beginning awareness of formal vs. informal, academic vs. casual registers
- [ ] Can use synonyms flexibly

### Grammar B2
- [ ] Secure command of all basic tenses and modal verbs
- [ ] Can use conditionals (first, second, third) accurately
- [ ] Can use subjunctive mood where required by language
- [ ] Can form and use passive voice naturally
- [ ] Can use a wide range of dependent clauses
- [ ] Few errors in complex structures; errors rarely impede communication

## C1 - Advanced (Effective Operational Proficiency)

**Overall Descriptor**: Can express themselves fluently and spontaneously without much obvious searching for expressions. Can use language flexibly and effectively for social, professional, and creative purposes. Can produce clear, well-structured, detailed text on complex subjects, showing controlled use of organizational patterns, connectors, and cohesive devices.

### Reading C1
- [ ] Can understand long, demanding texts on a wide range of topics
- [ ] Can understand implied meaning, nuance, and cultural references
- [ ] Can recognize subtle differences in style and tone
- [ ] Can follow complex academic, literary, or technical texts
- [ ] Can extract specific information from dense or abstract texts
- [ ] Can analyze argument structure and evaluate evidence

### Writing C1
- [ ] Can write well-structured, detailed texts on complex topics
- [ ] Can write academic essays with appropriate critical analysis
- [ ] Can write creatively (stories, poetry) with control of language
- [ ] Can write formal and informal correspondence with appropriate register and nuance
- [ ] Can revise and refine writing; can self-correct sophisticated errors
- [ ] Writing demonstrates sophisticated vocabulary and grammatical range

### Listening C1
- [ ] Can understand extended speech even when not clearly structured
- [ ] Can follow demanding audio (lectures, documentaries, debates) with difficulty mainly in unfamiliar topics
- [ ] Can recognize subtle shifts in tone and implication
- [ ] Can understand multiple speakers and complex arguments
- [ ] Can understand regional accents and different speech varieties
- [ ] Can catch humor, irony, and implicit meaning

### Speaking C1
- [ ] Can speak spontaneously and fluently without much searching for words
- [ ] Can use language flexibly to express nuance (doubt, emphasis, surprise)
- [ ] Can participate actively in complex discussions and debates
- [ ] Can express complex ideas with precision
- [ ] Can correct themselves naturally and repair misunderstandings
- [ ] Speech is smooth and natural; occasional hesitation for precision, not difficulty
- [ ] Can use different registers appropriately (academic, casual, professional)

### Vocabulary C1
- [ ] Vocabulary range: 3,500-5,000 words
- [ ] Wide and confident range across academic, technical, and literary language
- [ ] Excellent command of idiomatic expressions, colloquialisms, and regional variations
- [ ] Can understand and use specialized vocabulary in areas of expertise
- [ ] Can recognize and use words with subtle shades of meaning
- [ ] Can appreciate wordplay, puns, and literary devices

### Grammar C1
- [ ] Near-native control of grammar structures
- [ ] Can use rare or subtle grammatical constructions naturally
- [ ] Can manipulate grammar for stylistic effect
- [ ] Can use subjunctive mood with precision in all contexts
- [ ] Errors are rare and typically involve non-native word choices or very rare constructions
- [ ] Can explain grammatical rules and nuances

---

## C2 - Mastery (Mastery)

**Overall Descriptor**: Can understand with ease virtually everything heard or read. Can summarize information from different spoken and written sources, reconstructing arguments and accounts in a coherent presentation. Can express themselves spontaneously, very fluently, and precisely, differentiating finer shades of meaning even in more complex situations.

### Reading C2
- [ ] Can understand and interpret virtually all forms of written language
- [ ] Can read between the lines; understand subtle implications and irony
- [ ] Can extract precise information from dense, complex texts
- [ ] Can recognize nuances of style and appreciate literary merit
- [ ] Can follow complex academic, legal, or technical writing
- [ ] Can synthesize information across multiple complex texts

### Writing C2
- [ ] Can write with fluency, precision, and effectiveness on complex topics
- [ ] Can write in a variety of styles and registers with native-like appropriateness
- [ ] Can write creative fiction with sophisticated control of narrative and language
- [ ] Can write academic work at graduate/scholarly level
- [ ] Can use all varieties of punctuation and stylistic devices effectively
- [ ] Writing demonstrates near-native sophistication; errors are rare and minor

### Listening C2
- [ ] Can understand all varieties of the spoken language even at native speed
- [ ] Can understand regional dialects, slang, and colloquialisms
- [ ] Can catch subtle humor, irony, cultural references, and implied meaning
- [ ] Can understand technical and specialized lectures
- [ ] Can understand multiple speakers with overlapping speech
- [ ] Rarely misses information due to language; any difficulty is conceptual, not linguistic

### Speaking C2
- [ ] Can express themselves with native-like fluency and precision
- [ ] Can use language creatively and with sophistication
- [ ] Can participate equally in conversations with native speakers on any topic
- [ ] Can make fine distinctions in meaning using appropriately nuanced language
- [ ] Can convey subtle shades of emotion and irony
- [ ] Can use different registers and styles naturally and flexibly
- [ ] Speech is entirely natural; never struggles with expression

### Vocabulary C2
- [ ] Vocabulary range: 5,000+ words (approaching native speaker range of 10,000-15,000 active words)
- [ ] Complete command of rare and specialized vocabulary
- [ ] Can use idiomatic expressions with precision and recognize subtle variations
- [ ] Can appreciate and use archaic, regional, and technical language appropriately
- [ ] Can play with language (wordplay, puns, linguistic jokes)
- [ ] Can understand and use words in all their nuanced variations

### Grammar C2
- [ ] Near-perfect command of all grammatical structures
- [ ] Can manipulate grammar for rhetorical or literary effect
- [ ] Can use rare and archaic structures appropriately
- [ ] Errors, if they occur, are typically in highly specialized or rare constructions
- [ ] Can distinguish between grammatically correct and stylistically appropriate
- [ ] Can explain subtle grammatical distinctions

## Quick Reference: Progression Overview

| Level | Approximate Hours | Time Investment | Can Do |
|-------|-------------------|-----------------|---------|
| A1 | 200-250 hours | 3-4 months @ 2hrs/day | Greet, introduce self, handle basic survival situations |
| A2 | 300-400 hours | 5-6 months @ 2hrs/day | Describe daily life, handle routine travel, write simple messages |
| B1 | 400-500 hours | 6-8 months @ 2hrs/day | Discuss opinions, handle unexpected situations, tell stories |
| B2 | 500-600 hours | 8-10 months @ 2hrs/day | Debate, understand complex texts, write professional emails |
| C1 | 600-700 hours | 10-12 months @ 2hrs/day | Near-native fluency, subtle nuances, academic language |
| C2 | 700+ hours | 12+ months @ 2hrs/day | Complete mastery, native-like sophistication |

**Note**: These timelines assume:
- Consistent daily practice (best results at 1.5-2 hours/day)
- Mixed-method approach (Nation's Four Strands)
- Immersion-weighted curriculum at higher levels
- Language difficulty variations (Spanish faster for English speakers; Mandarin slower)

## Assessment Tips

When assessing a learner against these descriptors:

1. **Look for consistency**: A learner at B1 should be able to do most B1 tasks. If they can do C1 writing but A2 speaking, they have significant skill gaps.

2. **Consider production vs. recognition**: Many learners can recognize/understand a level above their productive ability. Note both receptive (reading/listening) and productive (writing/speaking) skills.

3. **Use real examples**: Don't just ask "Are you B1?" Ask learners to do actual B1 tasks (write about an opinion, discuss a topic) and assess the result against descriptors.

4. **Account for language distance**: A Spanish learner (close to English) may progress faster than a Mandarin learner. Adjust timeline expectations accordingly.

5. **Document gaps**: If a learner is B1 overall but A2 in grammar or vocabulary, document this. It guides targeted instruction.

6. **Explain clearly**: When sharing results with learners, cite these descriptors. Saying "You're B1 because you can describe experiences and opinions but struggle with complex texts" is more helpful and actionable than just "You're B1."

---

## Regional Variations

Note that CEFR is based primarily on European language standards. When assessing languages with significant regional variation (English: British, American, Indian; Spanish: European, Mexican, Argentine; Arabic: Modern Standard, Egyptian, Levantine), learners may reach higher levels in their target variety earlier.

Reference materials and tutoring should align with the learner's target variety when possible.
