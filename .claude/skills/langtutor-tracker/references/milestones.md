# CEFR Milestone Tracking Reference

This document defines the specific can-do statements, vocabulary targets, grammar targets, and estimated hours of study required to progress through each CEFR level transition. Use this reference when assessing whether a learner is ready to advance.

---

## A1 (Elementary Proficiency) → A2 (Elementary Proficiency - Upper)

**Estimated Study Hours:** 150-200 hours (varies by language family and prior study)

**Time for Typical Learner:** 8-12 weeks at 15-20 hours/week (most common trajectory)

### Can-Do Statements (Receptive Skills)
- Understand short, slowly-paced dialogues on familiar topics (family, school, work, hobbies)
- Recognize main points in brief written texts with high-frequency vocabulary
- Understand basic instructions and simple directions
- Identify topic of short listening passages (weather report, news headline, phone call)
- Understand common written notices (signs, simple emails, basic labels)
- Follow along when someone speaks slowly and clearly, using gestures and repetition
- Recognize familiar words and phrases in authentic but simplified material

### Can-Do Statements (Productive Skills)
- Introduce yourself and provide basic personal information (name, age, occupation, nationality)
- Ask simple questions about familiar topics and answer them
- Write simple messages and short postcards with correct basic grammar
- Describe in simple terms your hobbies, interests, and daily routine
- Make simple requests in face-to-face interactions ("Where is the bathroom?", "How much is this?")
- Participate in predictable social exchanges (greetings, farewells, polite formulas)
- Write short, simple texts about familiar subjects with some grammatical accuracy

### Can-Do Statements (Interactive Communication)
- Handle very simple face-to-face exchanges on basic topics
- Maintain a short conversation with native speaker who speaks slowly and clearly
- Ask for and provide clarification in simple terms
- Participate in routine exchanges (ordering food, buying tickets, checking into accommodation)

### Vocabulary Targets
- **Total words needed:** 500-700 words (active use)
- **Frequency breakdown:**
  - 200 core words (Tier 1): Personal pronouns, present tense verbs (be, have, do, go), basic nouns (family, home, food, animals)
  - 150 high-frequency words (Tier 2): Common adjectives (big, small, happy, sad), everyday verbs (like, want, need), time expressions
  - 150 situation-specific words: Food vocabulary, household items, clothing, basic greetings and social formulas
  - 100 comprehension-only words (passive recognition)

- **Key Vocabulary Topics:**
  - Personal identification (name, age, address, nationality, job)
  - Family and friends
  - Daily routine and activities
  - Food and drink
  - Clothing and appearance
  - House and home
  - Numbers and time
  - Basic colors and sizes
  - Common animals
  - Seasons and basic weather
  - Shopping and prices
  - Basic health and body parts

### Grammar Targets
- **Verb Systems:** Present tense conjugation (all persons) of regular verbs and ~10 common irregular verbs
- **Pronouns:** Personal, possessive, and reflexive pronouns (I/me/my, you/your, he/his, etc.)
- **Sentence Structure:** Simple present and present continuous tenses; basic subordination with "because" and "and"
- **Question Formation:** Yes/no questions and basic question words (who, what, where, when, how)
- **Negation:** Simple negative forms (not, don't, doesn't)
- **Determiners:** Articles (a/an/the or equivalent), demonstratives (this/that)
- **Prepositions:** Basic spatial prepositions (in, on, at, under, between)
- **Agreement:** Subject-verb agreement and gender agreement (if applicable to language)

### Evidence Requirements (What You'll See in Data)
- **Vocabulary:** ✓ 500+ words in vocab.json with average accuracy >85% on reviews
- **Session Performance:** ✓ Consistent >80% accuracy on A1-level conversation and grammar sessions
- **Listening:** ✓ Can identify main topic in 2-3 minute slow-paced dialogues (75%+ accuracy)
- **Reading:** ✓ Can answer basic comprehension questions about simple texts (80%+ accuracy)
- **Speaking:** ✓ Can produce 2-3 minute self-introduction and answer follow-up questions
- **Writing:** ✓ Can write 50-100 word email about daily routine with <5 grammar errors

### Readiness Checklist
- [ ] Vocabulary size: 500+ words, 85%+ accuracy on reviews
- [ ] Grammar strength: Consistent >85% on present tense, questions, negatives
- [ ] Listening comprehension: Can identify main points in slow-paced dialogues
- [ ] Reading comprehension: Can understand simple emails and text messages
- [ ] Speaking production: Can maintain 2+ minute unscripted conversation
- [ ] Writing production: Can write simple messages with acceptable grammar
- [ ] Motivation: Consistency in sessions (80%+ attendance past 2 weeks)
- [ ] Time investment: Minimum 150 hours completed (verify in sessions.json)

### Reassessment Recommendation Triggers
- Recommend formal reassessment through langtutor-assessor when:
  - All 6 can-do checkboxes above are complete
  - CEFR estimate reaches A2.0 or higher
  - Learner requests it ("I think I'm ready for the next level")
  - 2+ weeks of consistent performance suggest stability (not a lucky streak)

## A2 (Elementary) → B1 (Lower Intermediate)

**Estimated Study Hours:** 200-300 hours (cumulative from A1: 350-500 hours)

**Time for Typical Learner:** 12-16 weeks at 15-20 hours/week continuing from A2

### Can-Do Statements (Receptive Skills)
- Understand the main points of radio/TV news broadcasts and interviews on familiar topics
- Read simple articles and blog posts about personal interests with a dictionary
- Understand personal emails and correspondence from friends/colleagues
- Follow the main points in longer conversations between native speakers (with some repetition)
- Understand movies/TV shows with subtitles on familiar topics
- Recognize text organization in emails, formal letters, and simple news articles
- Infer meaning from context when some words are unfamiliar

### Can-Do Statements (Productive Skills)
- Write emails about personal matters and familiar topics (100-150 words) with correct grammar
- Describe past experiences and events in connected sentences
- Express opinions and justify them briefly ("I like X because...")
- Describe plans, intentions, and hypothetical situations
- Write simple narratives or summaries of events (past and future)
- Ask for and give detailed information in writing
- Use variety of tenses: past, present, future (some accuracy)

### Can-Do Statements (Interactive Communication)
- Initiate conversations with native speakers and maintain them beyond greetings
- Ask for clarification and paraphrase when needed to understand
- Exchange views and opinions in structured conversations
- Handle unexpected turns in conversation (being asked something new)
- Negotiate and discuss practical matters (travel plans, meeting times)
- Participate in discussions in group settings on familiar topics
- Deal with most situations and social interactions

### Vocabulary Targets
- **Total words needed:** 1,000-1,500 words (active use)
- **Addition from A2:** 500+ new words, with better depth of use (more synonyms, collocations)
- **Frequency breakdown:**
  - Core vocabulary: 800+ words used in 80% of everyday communication
  - Academic/formal vocabulary: 200+ words for written communication
  - Topic-specific vocabulary: Job-related, hobby-related, local culture-specific
  - Passive vocabulary: 500+ additional words for comprehension only

- **New Vocabulary Topics (Beyond A1):**
  - Work and employment (jobs, responsibilities, workplace vocabulary)
  - Education (school subjects, campus, learning-related)
  - Sports and recreation (active hobbies, team sports)
  - Health and medical (symptoms, doctor visits, basic medicine)
  - Travel (transportation, accommodation, tourist phrases)
  - Relationships and emotions (friendship, family dynamics, feelings)
  - Current events (news, politics, environment basics)
  - Technology (basic computer terms, common apps)
  - Customs and culture (traditions, holidays, social norms)

### Grammar Targets
- **Verb Systems:** Past tense (simple past and present perfect or equivalent), past continuous
- **Conditionals:** Basic first and second conditional (If X, then Y)
- **Reported Speech:** Ability to report what someone said ("He said that...")
- **Relative Clauses:** Basic relative clauses with who/that ("The person who...")
- **Passive Voice:** Recognition and basic production of passive forms
- **Complex Sentences:** Use of coordinating and subordinating conjunctions (because, although, while, etc.)
- **Indirect Questions:** "Do you know where the station is?"
- **Modal Verbs:** Can, could, must, should (and equivalents)
- **Article Usage:** More consistent and nuanced use of articles
- **Prepositions:** Expanded set including phrasal prepositions (in front of, next to)

### Evidence Requirements
- **Vocabulary:** ✓ 1,000+ words in vocab.json with 80%+ accuracy on spaced repetition reviews
- **Session Performance:** ✓ Consistent >80% accuracy on B1-level reading, >70% on writing tasks
- **Listening:** ✓ Can understand main ideas in authentic podcasts/videos (70%+ accuracy)
- **Reading:** ✓ Can read news articles and extract key information
- **Speaking:** ✓ Can maintain 5+ minute conversation with errors but good comprehension
- **Writing:** ✓ Can write 150-200 word emails/messages with mostly correct grammar
- **Past Tense:** ✓ Consistent use of past tense in speaking and writing (80%+ accuracy)

### Readiness Checklist
- [ ] Vocabulary size: 1,000+ words, 80%+ accuracy
- [ ] Past tense production: Can reliably use past tense in conversation and writing
- [ ] Reading comprehension: Can understand emails, articles, blog posts on familiar topics
- [ ] Listening comprehension: Can follow main points in conversations between native speakers
- [ ] Speaking fluency: Can maintain 5+ minute conversations with acceptable speed and accuracy
- [ ] Writing ability: Can compose emails and messages with correct grammar and appropriate length
- [ ] Grammar strength: Consistent use of past tense, conditionals, relative clauses (75%+ accuracy)
- [ ] Time investment: Minimum 350-500 hours completed (verify cumulative)

## B1 (Lower Intermediate) → B2 (Upper Intermediate)

**Estimated Study Hours:** 300-400 hours (cumulative from A1: 650-900 hours)

**Time for Typical Learner:** 16-24 weeks at 15-20 hours/week from B1 level

### Can-Do Statements (Receptive Skills)
- Understand main ideas and specific details in authentic videos and podcasts
- Read novels, complex articles, and specialized materials in area of interest
- Understand formal letters and professional documentation
- Follow arguments in TV documentaries and discussions, even when topic is unfamiliar
- Understand humor, irony, and implied meaning in authentic materials
- Extract information from multiple sources and synthesize understanding
- Understand accent variations and non-standard speech in authentic media

### Can-Do Statements (Productive Skills)
- Write formal letters and emails requesting information or explaining problems
- Express nuanced opinions and support with multiple reasons
- Write summaries and reviews of texts, films, or experiences (200+ words)
- Discuss complex topics with some accuracy and sophistication
- Adapt writing style to different contexts (formal/informal)
- Describe past experiences with fluency and narrative structure
- Explain abstract concepts and hypothetical situations

### Can-Do Statements (Interactive Communication)
- Have natural, spontaneous conversations without much preparation
- Express and defend opinions on abstract topics
- Participate actively in meetings and group discussions
- Handle unexpected conversation turns smoothly
- Understand and use social nuances (politeness, indirectness)
- Deal with complaints and negotiate effectively
- Build relationships and discuss personal feelings in depth

### Vocabulary Targets
- **Total words needed:** 2,000-2,500 words (active use)
- **Addition from B1:** 1,000+ new words including:
  - Academic/technical vocabulary in fields of interest
  - Sophisticated vocabulary for expressing nuance and abstraction
  - Idiomatic expressions and phrasal verbs
  - Synonyms and alternative ways to express same concept
  - Professional/business vocabulary
  - Passive vocabulary: 1,000+ additional words for comprehension

- **New Vocabulary Topics (Beyond B1):**
  - Professional/technical language (field-specific terminology)
  - Abstract concepts (philosophy, ethics, social issues)
  - Advanced social interactions (diplomacy, persuasion, negotiation)
  - Literature and arts (analysis, criticism, appreciation)
  - Specialized interests (science, business, culture, etc.)
  - Idioms and expressions with cultural context
  - Euphemisms and socially appropriate alternatives
  - Regional variations and dialect vocabulary

### Grammar Targets
- **Verb Systems:** Perfect tenses (present perfect continuous, past perfect), future forms (going to, will, present continuous for future)
- **Advanced Conditionals:** Third conditional, mixed conditionals, conditional inversion
- **Advanced Passive Voice:** More complex passive structures, reduced relative clauses
- **Gerunds and Infinitives:** Full control of gerunds vs. infinitives in all contexts
- **Subjunctive Moods:** Expressions of possibility, necessity, doubt
- **Complex Subordination:** Multiple clauses, embedded sentences, sophisticated conjunctions
- **Inversion and Emphasis:** Fronting structures, cleft sentences (optional by language)
- **Discourse Markers:** Sophisticated connectors (Furthermore, Nevertheless, On the other hand, etc.)
- **Stylistic Variation:** Ability to write and speak in different registers

### Evidence Requirements
- **Vocabulary:** ✓ 2,000+ words with 85%+ accuracy on reviews; can use synonyms and variations
- **Session Performance:** ✓ 75%+ accuracy on B2-level conversation, reading, writing tasks
- **Listening:** ✓ Can understand authentic podcast episodes with minimal repetition (80%+ accuracy)
- **Reading:** ✓ Can read and summarize novels and complex articles
- **Speaking:** ✓ Can hold 20+ minute conversations with natural flow and occasional hesitation
- **Writing:** ✓ Can write formal emails and essays (300+ words) with correct grammar and structure
- **Fluency:** ✓ Speaks with reasonable spontaneity; minimal searching for words
- **Sophistication:** ✓ Uses idioms, varied tenses, and nuanced expressions appropriately

### Readiness Checklist
- [ ] Vocabulary size: 2,000+ words, 85%+ accuracy; uses synonyms and variations
- [ ] Complex tenses: Confident use of perfect tenses, various conditionals
- [ ] Reading comprehension: Can read novels, complex articles, understand nuance
- [ ] Listening comprehension: Can follow authentic content with confidence
- [ ] Speaking fluency: Can hold long conversations with natural pacing and minimal hesitation
- [ ] Writing ability: Can write formal correspondence and multi-paragraph texts
- [ ] Idioms and expressions: Uses appropriate idioms and understands cultural context
- [ ] Time investment: Minimum 650+ hours cumulative (verify in data)

## B2 (Upper Intermediate) → C1 (Advanced Proficiency)

**Estimated Study Hours:** 400-600 hours (cumulative from A1: 1,050-1,500 hours)

**Time for Typical Learner:** 24-32 weeks at 15-20 hours/week from B2

### Can-Do Statements (Receptive Skills)
- Understand implicit meaning, undertones, and cultural references in authentic materials
- Read specialized academic texts and technical documentation in area of expertise
- Understand nuance and subtlety in native speaker dialogue and literature
- Follow fast-paced, colloquial speech including slang and regional variations
- Understand irony, sarcasm, and wordplay in authentic media
- Analyze and critique texts, arguments, and perspectives
- Extract complex information from multiple authentic sources and synthesize

### Can-Do Statements (Productive Skills)
- Produce well-structured, formal texts (reports, academic papers, proposals) without preparation
- Express complex ideas with precision and nuance
- Write creatively (stories, poetry) with control of style and tone
- Speak with fluency and spontaneity, matching speed and register to audience
- Defend positions with sophisticated argumentation
- Use language creatively and effectively to persuade or influence
- Adapt communication to different audiences and contexts seamlessly

### Can-Do Statements (Interactive Communication)
- Participate comfortably in debates and academic discussions
- Handle complex negotiations with linguistic precision
- Understand and respond appropriately to indirect communication and implication
- Build sophisticated relationships through language
- Use language strategically and tactfully in sensitive situations
- Understand and use cultural-specific communication styles
- Maintain coherent discourse across multiple turns and complex topics

### Vocabulary Targets
- **Total words needed:** 3,500-4,500 words (active use)
- **Addition from B2:** 2,000+ advanced words:
  - Academic/specialized vocabulary in multiple fields
  - Rare and sophisticated synonyms
  - Advanced phrasal verbs and collocations
  - Figurative language and metaphor
  - Register-specific vocabulary (formal, informal, technical, literary)
  - Passive vocabulary: 2,000+ additional words

- **Advanced Vocabulary Areas:**
  - Discipline-specific terminology (your field(s) of interest)
  - Advanced academic discourse markers
  - Sophisticated literary vocabulary
  - Technical jargon beyond basic proficiency
  - Euphemisms and diplomatic language
  - Rare and archaic expressions (for recognition)
  - Words with subtle meaning differences (nuanced synonyms)

### Grammar Targets
- **Mastery Level:** Near-native-like control of all major grammar structures
- **Advanced Passive Voice:** Mixed voice, passive with different subjects, stylistic passive
- **Complex Subordination:** Nested clauses, multiple embeddings, sophisticated conjunctions
- **Subjunctive and Conditional Moods:** Full control in all contexts
- **Stylistic Transformations:** Ability to express same idea in multiple grammatically correct ways
- **Register Variation:** Conscious manipulation of formality and style through grammar choices
- **Rare Structures:** Inversions, cleft sentences, reduced clauses used appropriately
- **Discourse Structure:** Control of information flow, given/new information, emphasis

### Evidence Requirements
- **Vocabulary:** ✓ 3,500+ active words with 90%+ accuracy; masters sophisticated synonyms
- **Session Performance:** ✓ 80%+ accuracy on C1-level reading, discussion, writing tasks
- **Listening:** ✓ Can understand fast-paced, colloquial speech with native-like comprehension
- **Reading:** ✓ Can read academic texts, literature, and technical documentation with critical analysis
- **Speaking:** ✓ Fluent speech with native-like spontaneity; can pause to find right word but rarely searches for ideas
- **Writing:** ✓ Can write academic or professional texts (500+ words) with sophisticated structure
- **Nuance:** ✓ Understands and uses irony, sarcasm, cultural references, idioms
- **Precision:** ✓ Uses precise, sophisticated vocabulary; masters collocations and expressions

### Readiness Checklist
- [ ] Vocabulary size: 3,500+ words, 90%+ accuracy; uses sophisticated synonyms and collocations
- [ ] Grammar mastery: Near-native-like control of all structures; can vary style
- [ ] Reading comprehension: Can read academic, literary, and technical texts with analysis
- [ ] Listening comprehension: Can follow native speakers at natural speed and pace
- [ ] Speaking fluency: Near-native fluency; rapid word retrieval; can discuss abstract topics
- [ ] Writing sophistication: Can produce academic/professional texts with sophisticated structure
- [ ] Cultural understanding: Understands irony, sarcasm, cultural references, idioms
- [ ] Time investment: Minimum 1,050+ hours cumulative

## C1 (Advanced) → C2 (Mastery)

**Estimated Study Hours:** 600+ hours (cumulative from A1: 1,650+ hours)

**Time for Typical Learner:** 32+ weeks at 15-20 hours/week from C1; often requires focused study of rare materials

### Can-Do Statements (Receptive Skills)
- Understand all nuances in native speaker dialogue including regional dialect, slang, accent variation
- Read all authentic materials including dialect literature, experimental writing, specialized academic texts
- Understand implicit cultural and historical references without explanation
- Grasp subtle distinctions between synonymous expressions
- Understand humor, irony, and wordplay in all contexts
- Analyze language use itself (grammatical, stylistic, rhetorical choices)
- Near-native listening comprehension in all contexts

### Can-Do Statements (Productive Skills)
- Express ideas with complete fluency and precision comparable to educated native speakers
- Write creatively with control of style, tone, and artistic effect
- Produce academic, technical, and professional texts at expert level
- Speak spontaneously at native-like speed and with native-like precision
- Use language to persuade, influence, and affect listeners at sophisticated level
- Code-switch and play with language in creative ways
- Adapt communication perfectly to any audience or context

### Can-Do Statements (Interactive Communication)
- Participate fully in intellectual discussions at native-speaker level
- Negotiate complex matters with near-native linguistic sophistication
- Use language for creative and artistic expression (poetry, storytelling, humor)
- Understand and navigate complex social hierarchies and indirect communication
- Mentor or teach others in the language (on the language's terms, not as a teacher)
- Engage in professional academic or literary discourse

### Vocabulary Targets
- **Total words needed:** 5,000+ words (active use)
- **Addition from C1:** 1,500+ words of highest-frequency rare materials:
  - Specialized vocabulary in fields you work/study in at expert level
  - Literary and archaic vocabulary for recognition and appreciation
  - Rare synonyms and nuanced distinctions
  - Dialect-specific vocabulary
  - Slang and colloquial expressions from multiple regions
  - Technical jargon in specialized fields
  - Passive vocabulary: 2,000+ additional words from specialized texts

- **C2 Vocabulary Focus:**
  - Mastery-level vocabulary in one or more specialized fields
  - Recognition of archaic and literary vocabulary
  - Regional vocabulary variants and dialects
  - Highly sophisticated expressions and rare collocations
  - Ability to coin new expressions or use old ones in novel ways

### Grammar Targets
- **Near-Native Mastery:** Control comparable to educated native speakers
- **Full Range:** Can use all grammatical structures appropriately
- **Stylistic Sophistication:** Uses grammar consciously for style, emphasis, and artistic effect
- **Linguistic Analysis:** Can discuss grammar and usage (metalinguistic awareness)
- **Exception Handling:** Understands and can appropriately use exceptions and rare structures
- **Register Mastery:** Complete control across all registers and contexts
- **Creative Use:** Can violate grammar rules intentionally for effect (like native speakers do)

### Evidence Requirements
- **Vocabulary:** ✓ 5,000+ words mastered; can read and understand specialized literature
- **Session Performance:** ✓ 85%+ accuracy on C2-level materials (often mastery is demonstrated through absence of errors rather than high percentage)
- **Listening:** ✓ Native-like comprehension in all contexts including accents, slang, colloquial speech
- **Reading:** ✓ Can read and analyze all authentic materials including literary, academic, technical
- **Speaking:** ✓ Indistinguishable from educated native speaker in most contexts
- **Writing:** ✓ Can write at expert level in any genre (creative, academic, professional, journalistic)
- **Native-Like Precision:** ✓ Uses language with precision and sophistication comparable to educated native speakers
- **Linguistic Awareness:** ✓ Can reflect on language choices and discuss why expressions work

### Readiness Checklist
- [ ] Vocabulary: 5,000+ active words; masters specialized and rare vocabulary
- [ ] Grammar: Near-native control across all structures and registers
- [ ] Reading: Can read and analyze all authentic materials (literary, academic, dialect)
- [ ] Listening: Native-like comprehension in all contexts including rapid, colloquial speech
- [ ] Speaking: Indistinguishable from educated native speaker for most interactions
- [ ] Writing: Can write at expert level in any genre with sophistication
- [ ] Precision: Masters subtle distinctions in meaning and usage
- [ ] Time investment: Minimum 1,650+ hours cumulative (often requires 2,000+ hours)

### Note on C2
C2 represents complete mastery comparable to educated native speakers. Reaching this level typically requires:
- Deep immersion or sustained residence in target language environment
- Focused study of specialized fields or literature
- Years of dedicated practice (not weeks or months)
- For most learners, C1 represents a realistic and valuable endpoint
- C2 pursuit is usually motivated by professional, academic, or passionate reasons

## Milestone Assessment Guidelines

### How to Use This Reference

When analyzing a learner's progress in progress.json:

1. **Check Against Current Level:** Find the learner's current CEFR level (e.g., A2.3)
2. **Read Relevant Section:** Look at the next level milestone (A2 → B1)
3. **Compare Evidence:** Check sessions.json and vocab.json against the "Evidence Requirements"
4. **Count Completed Items:** Use the "Readiness Checklist" to see how many criteria are met
5. **Calculate Completeness:** (Completed items ÷ Total items) × 100 = % ready for advancement
6. **Make Recommendation:**
   - **0-25%:** "You're making great progress. Keep practicing; advancement in [X weeks]"
   - **25-50%:** "You're mid-way through [Level]. Focus on [specific weak areas]; advancement in [X weeks]"
   - **50-75%:** "You're close to [Level]. A few more weeks of focused practice on [areas]"
   - **75-100%:** "You're ready for a formal reassessment. Shall we schedule one?"

### Adjustments by Language Family

These timelines assume Romance languages (Spanish, French, Italian, Portuguese) or Germanic languages (German, Dutch) for English speakers.

**Adjustments:**
- **Easier languages** (similar to learner's native language): Reduce estimated hours by 20-30%
- **Harder languages** (Chinese, Arabic, Finnish, Hungarian): Increase estimated hours by 30-50%
- **FSI Official Estimates:**
  - Category I (Easy): 600-750 hours for English speakers (Spanish, French, Dutch, Portuguese)
  - Category II (Medium): 900-1,100 hours (German, Icelandic, Swedish)
  - Category III (Hard): 2,200+ hours (Arabic, Chinese, Japanese, Korean)

### Using Historical Data

If you have session.json with timestamps:
- Calculate actual hours invested
- Compare actual pace to estimated timeline
- If learner is 50% faster: May reach milestones sooner (could be exceptional talent OR overestimating current level)
- If learner is 50% slower: May need more time (but consistency matters—slow steady progress often beats fast plateaus)

### Individual Differences

Remember:
- These are estimates. Individual learners vary significantly.
- Motivation, study method, and immersion level matter more than time
- Some learners plateau for weeks then breakthrough quickly
- Consistency beats intensity (1 hour daily > 7 hours on one day)
- Natural talent exists but good method beats raw talent

Always be encouraging while honest about timeline.
