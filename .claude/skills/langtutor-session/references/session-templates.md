# Session Templates Reference: langtutor-session

Detailed templates for all session types across all CEFR levels (A1-C2). Use these as blueprints for session design and execution.

## VOCABULARY BUILDER TEMPLATES

### A1 Vocabulary Builder: Daily Routines (10-minute session)

**Objective**: Learn 6 words related to morning/evening routines with simple example sentences

**Vocabulary to introduce**:
1. Wake up (despertarse/se réveiller/起床)
2. Breakfast (desayuno/petit-déjeuner/早餐)
3. Brush teeth (cepillarse los dientes/se brosser les dents/刷牙)
4. Shower (ducha/douche/洗澡)
5. Get dressed (vestirse/s'habiller/穿衣服)
6. Go to bed (acostarse/se coucher/睡觉)

**Warm-up** (1 minute):
- Show pictures of morning activities
- Ask in English: "What do you do when you wake up?"
- Response primes the learner's thinking

**Introduction** (4 minutes):
Each word presented as: Visual + Phrase + Example Sentence + Pronunciation
- Show image of person waking up
- Say and write: "DESPERTARSE = to wake up"
- Phonetic: "des-per-TAR-seh"
- Example: "Me despierto a las 7 de la mañana" (I wake up at 7 in the morning)
- Repeat 3 times, have learner repeat

Repeat pattern for each of 6 words, showing physical actions when possible

**Encoding** (3 minutes):
- Learner repeats all 6 words in sequence
- Show pictures in random order; learner says word
- Learner creates one example sentence using 2 words: "Me despierto y tomo desayuno"
- Quick memory game: "I'll say a word in English, you say it in [target language]"

**Production** (2 minutes):
- Learner describes their morning routine using all 6 words
- Accept short sentences: "Despierto. Douche. Desayuno. Vestido. Trabajo."
- Praise all attempts

**Close**: "Great! You learned 6 new words today. Tomorrow we'll practice these with more details."

### A2 Vocabulary Builder: Restaurant Menu (15-minute session)

**Objective**: Learn 12 food/drink words and practice ordering at a restaurant

**Vocabulary to introduce**:
- Appetizers: entrante/entrée, sopa/soupe
- Main courses: pollo/poulet (chicken), carne/viande (meat), pescado/poisson (fish)
- Vegetables: verduras/légumes, patatas/pommes de terre, arroz/riz
- Drinks: agua/eau, vino/vin, cerveza/bière
- Dessert: postre/dessert

**Warm-up** (1.5 minutes):
- Show restaurant menu image
- Recall 3 foods from previous sessions
- Quick: "What do you like to eat?"

**Introduction** (5 minutes):
For each word: image + written word + pronunciation + example in mini-context
- "POLLO (POH-yoh) = chicken. Example: Quiero pollo." (I want chicken)
- Show word in context of menu, not isolated
- Group by course (appetizers, mains, drinks, desserts)

**Encoding** (4 minutes):
- Repeat all 12 words with images
- Matching game: show image, learner names food
- Gap-fill: "Para comer, quiero un plato de ____" (For eating, I want a plate of ___)
- Learner chooses favorite from menu and states preference: "Me gusta el pollo"

**Production** (3 minutes):
- Learner "orders" from menu using 3-4 words in a sentence
- Accept: "Quiero pollo, verduras, y agua" (I want chicken, vegetables, and water)
- You ask follow-up: "¿Con vino?" (With wine?) → learner responds yes/no

**Close**: "Excellent! Next time we'll practice a full restaurant conversation using these words."

### B1 Vocabulary Builder: Academic Topics (20-minute session)

**Objective**: Learn 12-15 words related to university/education for use in B1 discourse

**Vocabulary to introduce**:
- Subjects: filosofía/philosophie, ingeniería/ingénierie, biología/biologie
- Academic life: matrícula/inscription (enrollment), calificación/note (grade), apuntes/notes
- Actions: estudiar/étudier, entregar/remettre (submit), suspender/échouer (fail)
- Abstract concepts: teoría/théorie, hipótesis/hypothèse, conclusión/conclusion

**Warm-up** (1.5 minutes):
- Discuss learner's field of study or interest
- Activate schema: "Tell me in English what subject you're interested in"

**Introduction** (6 minutes):
Present in semantic clusters (not individual words):
- CLUSTER 1 - Academic subjects (with cognates highlighted): "Many are similar in English..."
- CLUSTER 2 - School activities: show video clip of university life
- CLUSTER 3 - Abstract concepts: provide visual representations or diagrams

Each word: pronunciation + definition in target language + English clarification + context sentence

**Encoding** (6 minutes):
- Semantic mapping: learner arranges words by category on virtual map
- Cognate discovery: "Which of these words look similar to English?"
- Example sentence generation: learner writes sentence using 1-2 new words from each cluster
- Context exercise: Read a short passage (150 words) about university life, identify new vocabulary

**Production** (5 minutes):
- Learner writes short paragraph (5-7 sentences) about their own studies using 8+ new words
- Or: Learner answers questions: "What subjects interest you? Why? What's your best grade?"
- Encourage complex sentences: "I'm studying engineering because I want to design bridges"

**Close**: "You've built a strong academic vocabulary foundation. Practice these with reading materials next."

### B2 Vocabulary Builder: Idioms and Expressions (20-minute session)

**Objective**: Learn 8-10 idioms expressing emotions, situations, or abstract concepts

**Note**: At B2, vocabulary learning focuses on nuance, idioms, and expressions rather than basic vocabulary

**Vocabulary to introduce**:
- "Estar en la luna" (Spanish) = to be daydreaming (literally: "be on the moon")
- "Avoir le cafard" (French) = to feel down (literally: "have the cockroach")
- "Mettre les pieds dans le plat" (French) = to put your foot in your mouth
- "Tener una cara de vinagre" (Spanish) = to have a sour face (be unhappy)
- "Ne pas être dans son assiette" (French) = not to be feeling well (not in one's plate)

**Warm-up** (1 minute):
- Discuss English idioms: "What idioms do you use?"
- Bridge: "Every language has colorful expressions. Let's explore Spanish/French ones"

**Introduction** (4 minutes):
For each idiom:
1. Literal translation: show the words mean
2. Actual meaning: explain what it really means
3. Cultural context: why did this expression develop?
4. Usage context: when/where it's appropriate

Example: "Estar en la luna means literally 'to be on the moon'. We use it when someone isn't paying attention. You might say this about someone daydreaming in class."

**Encoding** (6 minutes):
- Learner matches idiom to meaning (multiple choice)
- Learner explains idiom to you, you correct/clarify
- Watch authentic video clip (movie scene) showing idiom in context
- Learner guesses idiom from situational description: "Your friend forgot your birthday. What idiom describes your feeling?" → "tener cara de vinagre"

**Production** (7 minutes):
- Learner creates dialogue using 3-4 idioms appropriately
- Or: Learner responds to emotional situations with idioms: "You got the job! How would you say this in [language]?"
- Learner translates their own emotional expression from English to target language idiom

**Close**: "These idioms help you sound like a native speaker. Use them in our next conversation practice!"

### C1 Vocabulary Builder: Formal Academic Discourse (25-minute session)

**Objective**: Master 10-12 words for sophisticated academic/professional communication

**Vocabulary to introduce**:
- "Conjetura/conjecture" - supposition without proof
- "Validar/valider" - confirm through evidence
- "Refutar/réfuter" - disprove with evidence
- "Paradigma/paradigme" - fundamental model or framework
- "Implicación/implication" - logical consequence
- "Ambigüedad/ambiguité" - double meaning
- "Coherencia/cohérence" - logical consistency
- "Argumento/argument" - logical reasoning chain

**Warm-up** (1 minute):
- Discuss: "In academic writing, word choice matters enormously. Today we'll learn words used in research and debate."

**Introduction** (5 minutes):
Present through authentic context:
- Show excerpt from academic journal (same language)
- Highlight the 8 words in context
- Discuss how each word functions in academic argument
- Compare to simpler synonyms: "You could say 'think' but scholars say 'conjecture'"

**Encoding** (8 minutes):
- Learner reads 2-3 sentence definitions in target language
- Learner finds examples of each word in academic texts (short articles provided)
- Create semantic network: how do these words relate? (conjecture → validation/refutation; ambiguity → clarity → coherence)
- Learner creates example sentences in academic context

**Production** (9 minutes):
- Learner writes short critical response to a position paper (150+ words) using 6+ new words
- Or: Learner engages in mini-debate where they must use these words appropriately
- Or: Learner explains academic concept, then revises to use more sophisticated vocabulary from this session

**Close**: "You now have vocabulary for sophisticated academic communication. Your next reading task will use these words extensively."

### C2 Vocabulary Builder: Nuanced Registers and Style (30-minute session)

**Objective**: Master subtle stylistic variations and register-specific vocabulary

**Focus**: Not learning new concepts, but learning how native speakers vary language for effect, audience, context

**Vocabulary to introduce**:
- Formal register variations: "decir/manifestar/expresar/señalar" (say - with increasing formality)
- Archaic vs. modern: "pues/ahora bien/en fin" (discourse markers with historical layers)
- Regional variants: Spanish (España vs. México vs. Argentina) or French (France vs. Belgium)
- Ironic/sarcastic variations
- Literary/poetic language: metaphor, symbolism, archetype

**Warm-up** (2 minutes):
- Discuss: "Native speakers code-switch—they change how they speak for different audiences. Let's explore the subtleties."

**Introduction** (6 minutes):
Present through authentic texts showing same idea in different registers:
- Informal text (social media)
- Standard text (news)
- Formal text (academic)
- Literary text (novel)
Highlight vocabulary choices that signal register

**Encoding** (10 minutes):
- Learner analyzes register choices in provided texts
- Learner rewrites same paragraph in 3 different registers
- Learner listens to natives expressing same idea at different registers, identifies vocabulary shifts
- Create spectrum showing formality gradient for each concept

**Production** (10 minutes):
- Learner writes monologue for 3 different contexts (casual chat, job interview, literary essay) on same topic, varying register appropriately
- Or: Learner analyzes a famous speaker/writer's vocabulary choices for effect
- Or: Learner conducts interview using appropriate register with different "character" personas

**Close**: "You've reached true mastery of nuance. These subtle choices distinguish C2 speakers."

## GRAMMAR IN CONTEXT TEMPLATES

### A1 Grammar in Context: Present Tense Basic (10-minute session)

**Objective**: Discover and practice present tense "I/you/he-she" forms

**Grammar point**: Regular present tense conjugation (hablar/parler/speak)

**Warm-up** (1 minute):
- Show three images: person speaking, two people speaking, person listening
- Say: "Watch for pattern"

**Inductive Discovery** (3 minutes):
Present 6 example sentences (no explanation):
1. "Yo hablo español" (I speak Spanish)
2. "Tú hablas español" (You speak Spanish)
3. "Él habla español" (He speaks Spanish)
4. "Ella habla español" (She speaks Spanish)
5. "Nosotros hablamos español" (We speak Spanish)
6. "Ellos hablan español" (They speak Spanish)

Ask: "What changes? What stays the same? Can you guess what 'hablas' means?"

**Pattern Recognition** (2 minutes):
Guide learner to notice:
- Root "habl-" stays the same
- Ending changes: -o, -as, -a, -amos, -an
- Ending tells you WHO is doing the action

**Communicative Function** (1 minute):
"This helps you talk about what people do. You can say what YOU do, what YOUR FRIEND does, what HE/SHE does"

**Controlled Practice** (2 minutes):
Gap-fill with person image cues:
- "Yo _____ inglés" (I ___ English) → hablo
- "Ella _____ francés" (She ___ French) → habla
- "Nosotros _____ español" (We ___ Spanish) → hablamos

**Free Practice** (1 minute):
"Tell me: Do you speak English? Spanish? What does your teacher speak?"
Accept: "Yo hablo inglés y español"

**Error handling**: If learner says "Yo hablas", recast: "Right, you're learning—we say 'Yo hablo'. Good effort!"

### A2 Grammar in Context: Past Tense Introduction (15-minute session)

**Objective**: Learn basic past tense (pretérito/passé composé) for simple narratives

**Grammar point**: Regular past tense, simple form

**Warm-up** (1.5 minutes):
- Tell simple personal story in past tense in target language, using gestures for temporal reference
- "Hoy me desperté a las 7. Desayuné. Fui al trabajo."
- Ask: "Can you spot the pattern? These describe things that already happened."

**Inductive Discovery** (4 minutes):
Show 5 parallel sentences (present and past):
- Present: "Hablo español" | Past: "Hablé español"
- Present: "Como pizza" | Past: "Comí pizza"
- Present: "Vives aquí" | Past: "Viviste aquí"
- Present: "Beben café" | Past: "Bebieron café"

Point out: "Notice: one word tells us NOW, the other tells us BEFORE"

Ask: "What changed? Just the ending, right? The root stayed the same"

**Pattern Recognition** (2 minutes):
Guide discovery:
- Regular past endings: -é/-aste/-ó/-amos/-asteis/-aron (Spanish)
- Contrast with present: Present ends in -o/-as/-a, Past ends in -é/-aste/-ó
- Timeline: We use past tense for complete, finished actions

**Communicative Function** (1.5 minutes):
"This helps you tell stories about what you did yesterday, last week, your childhood. Really important for real conversation."

**Controlled Practice** (4 minutes):
- Transform present to past (5 sentences): "Como manzanas" → "Comí manzanas"
- Fill in past tense based on image context: "Ayer [I ate] una hamburguesa"
- Listen to story, mark whether actions are present or past

**Free Practice** (2 minutes):
"Tell me 3 things you did yesterday"
Learner produces: "Comí desayuno. Trabajé. Hablé con mi amiga."

**Error handling**: Learner says "Yo comé" (wrong accent), recast: "You're on the right track—'Comí' is what we say for 'I ate'"

### B1 Grammar in Context: Subjunctive Mood (20-minute session)

**Objective**: Understand subjunctive mood and when to use it (expressing doubt, desire, influence)

**Grammar point**: Present subjunctive in subordinate clauses

**Warm-up** (1.5 minutes):
- Discuss: "Some languages have 'moods' that show your attitude toward something. English uses words like 'I want', 'I doubt', 'I suggest'. Spanish/French uses special verb forms."
- Contrast: "It IS raining" (fact) vs. "I hope it doesn't rain" (wish)

**Inductive Discovery** (5 minutes):
Present parallel sentences showing indicative vs. subjunctive:
- INDICATIVE (fact): "Sé que vienes mañana" (I know that you're coming tomorrow) - CERTAIN
- SUBJUNCTIVE (desire): "Quiero que vengas mañana" (I want you to come tomorrow) - DEPENDENT ON WILL
- INDICATIVE (fact): "Creo que es posible" (I believe it's possible) - SURE
- SUBJUNCTIVE (doubt): "Dudo que sea posible" (I doubt it's possible) - UNCERTAIN

Pattern to discover:
- When you're CERTAIN/SURE → use normal (indicative) form
- When you WISH/DOUBT/WANT something → use special (subjunctive) form
- The verb form changes shape

**Pattern Recognition** (3 minutes):
Guide learner to identify:
- Triggers for subjunctive: verbs of desire (quiero), doubt (dudo), emotion (espero), influence (insisto)
- Form changes: regular endings shift (-ar→-e, -er→-a)
- Only subjunctive in the second clause: "Yo quiero QUE TÚ vengas" (the 'que' is the trigger)

**Communicative Function** (1.5 minutes):
"You need this to express wishes, recommendations, doubts, and when influencing others. Very common in real speech."

**Controlled Practice** (6 minutes):
- Identify correct form: "Espero QUE (vienes/vengas) a mi fiesta" → vengas
- Create sentences from prompts: "I want you to study" → "Quiero que estudies"
- Match trigger phrase to appropriate subjunctive response

**Free Practice** (3 minutes):
"Tell me three things you want your friend to do. Use 'Quiero que...'"
Accept: "Quiero que estudies. Quiero que hables español. Quiero que vengas a mi casa."

**Error handling**: Learner says "Quiero que vienes", recast: "Close! After 'quiero que', we use a special form: 'Quiero que vengas'. The subjunctive shows it's your wish, not a fact."

### B2 Grammar in Context: Conditional and Hypotheticals (20-minute session)

**Objective**: Master conditional forms and hypothetical structures for sophisticated expression

**Grammar point**: Conditional + Past Subjunctive for unreal situations

**Warm-up** (1.5 minutes):
- Discuss: "We often imagine 'what if' situations. Native speakers express hypotheticals with specific grammar."
- Give examples: "If I won the lottery..." (hypothetical)

**Inductive Discovery** (5 minutes):
Present pairs showing real vs. hypothetical:
- REAL (If it rains, I'll cancel) → "Si llueve, cancelaré"
- HYPOTHETICAL (If it rained, I would cancel) → "Si lloviera, cancelaría"
- PAST HYPOTHETICAL (If it had rained, I would have canceled) → "Si hubiera llovido, habría cancelado"

Ask: "Notice: different tense combinations signal whether this is POSSIBLE or IMPOSSIBLE. What pattern do you see?"

**Pattern Recognition** (4 minutes):
Guide discovery of:
- Real conditional: Si + present → will/future
- Hypothetical conditional: Si + past subjunctive → would/conditional
- Past hypothetical: Si + pluperfect subjunctive → would have + past participle
- Form changes: llovería (would rain), hubiera llovido (had rained)

**Communicative Function** (1.5 minutes):
"This is how you discuss dreams, regrets, negotiations, and imaginary situations. Essential for adult conversation."

**Controlled Practice** (5 minutes):
- Identify real vs. hypothetical: "Si tengo dinero, compraré..." vs. "Si tuviera dinero, compraría..."
- Transform: "If he comes, I'll explain" → Spanish conditional form
- Complete hypothetical sentences: "Si pudiera viajar, ___"

**Free Practice** (3 minutes):
"Tell me three hypothetical situations"
Accept: "Si ganara la lotería, viajaría. Si tuviera tiempo, estudiaría más. Si hablara perfectamente, sería profesor."

### C1 Grammar in Context: Stylistic Inversion and Advanced Syntax (25-minute session)

**Objective**: Master stylistic variations in clause ordering, inversions, and complex structures

**Grammar point**: Variation in word order for rhetorical effect, advanced subordination

**Warm-up** (2 minutes):
- Discuss: "Proficient speakers vary word order for emphasis and style. Your teacher said X; a novel writer might say it as Y. Both are correct, but create different effects."

**Inductive Discovery** (6 minutes):
Present authentic examples from literature/journalism showing variation:
- STANDARD: "El detective descubrió la verdad" (The detective discovered the truth)
- INVERTED FOR EMPHASIS: "La verdad, la descubrió el detective" (The truth, the detective discovered it)
- LITERARY INVERSION: "Descubrió el detective la verdad entre las sombras" (Discovered the detective the truth among shadows)

Pattern to discover:
- Moving elements changes emphasis
- Passivized structures create distance/formality
- Subordination order affects meaning prominence

**Pattern Recognition** (4 minutes):
Guide learning of:
- Fronting (moving object to front for emphasis)
- Passivization (using passive voice for stylistic effect)
- Cleft sentences ("It was X that...")
- Suspended constructions (holding main idea until end)

**Communicative Function** (1.5 minutes):
"Understanding these structures helps you read literature, academic writing, journalism. Producing them marks you as highly proficient."

**Controlled Practice** (7 minutes):
- Read example sentences; explain WHY author chose that structure
- Rewrite sentences in different orders; discuss effect change
- Identify stylistic inversion in provided authentic texts

**Free Practice** (4 minutes):
- Learner writes short narrative paragraph, then rewrites it using 2-3 stylistic inversions for literary effect
- Discuss effect: "By fronting the object, I created mystery. By passivizing, I created distance."

### C2 Grammar in Context: Dialectal and Register Variation (30-minute session)

**Objective**: Understand and produce grammatical variations across dialects and registers

**Grammar point**: Non-standard variations, historical forms, regional grammar

**Warm-up** (2 minutes):
- Discuss: "Native speakers aren't monolithic. Regional dialects, generations, and social contexts create grammatical variation. C2 means understanding ALL variations."

**Inductive Discovery** (6 minutes):
Show same grammatical content in different varieties:
- EUROPEAN SPANISH: "Os digo que vengáis" (I tell you [formal plural] to come)
- LATIN AMERICAN: "Les digo que vengan" (I tell you to come)
- DIFFERENT TENSE: "Vosotros vinisteis" (You all came - European) vs. "Ustedes vinieron" (You came - everywhere else)

Ask: "These say the same thing. What changed? Which would you use where?"

**Pattern Recognition** (5 minutes):
Guide learning of:
- Geographic variation: Spain vs. Americas; France vs. Belgium/Quebec
- Generational difference: older speakers use forms younger speakers don't
- Social class markers: "correct" grammar vs. casual speech
- Historical archaisms: forms no longer used but found in literature

**Communicative Function** (2 minutes):
"Understanding variation lets you appreciate literature across centuries and regions. Producing native-like speech means knowing which variety fits your context."

**Controlled Practice** (10 minutes):
- Learner reads passages representing different varieties, identifies features
- Learner watches media from different countries, notes grammatical differences
- Learner rewrites same dialogue for different regional contexts

**Free Practice** (5 minutes):
- Learner produces extended discourse (3-4 minutes) analyzing grammatical variation they've observed in native media
- Or: Learner demonstrates understanding of dialect-specific grammar through adapted dialogue

## CONVERSATION SIMULATION TEMPLATES

### A1 Conversation: Greetings and Introductions (10-minute session)

**Scenario**: You're meeting someone new at a cafe

**Your role**: Friendly stranger at a nearby table
**Learner role**: Yourself, a visitor to this country

**Setup** (1 minute):
- "You're sitting at a café. I'm a local person who notices you're new. We greet each other naturally. I'll speak slowly and clearly. If you don't understand, ask me 'repeat please' or 'slow down please'. Don't worry—mistakes are good. Let's go!"

**Island of safety** (learner can use anytime):
- "Repeat, please"
- "Speak slowly, please"
- "I don't understand"
- "What does _____ mean?"
- "In English?"

**Conversation flow**:

YOU: [Friendly greeting in TL] "Hello! How are you?"
LEARNER: [Expected: simple response] "Hello, I'm fine"
YOU: [Bridging - provide word if needed] "Nice. Where are you from?"
LEARNER: [Expected: country name] "I am from America"
YOU: "How long are you here?" [If silent, say: "One week? One month?"]
LEARNER: [Pick option or provide own] "One week"
YOU: "What do you think of our city?" [Bridging if needed]
LEARNER: "It's beautiful!" or "It's very nice"
YOU: "Great! What's your name?"
LEARNER: [Their name]
YOU: "Nice to meet you! [Student name]"

**Feedback** (2 minutes):
Positive: "You understood and answered every question! You said 'repeat please' naturally. You pronounced [word] beautifully."
Improvement: "Next time, try making a complete sentence: instead of just 'America', try 'I am from America'. Or even: 'I am from America. I am here one week.'"

**Reflection** (1 minute):
- "How did that feel?"
- "What was easy? What was hard?"
- Next session preview: "Next we'll practice ordering food at that café. You'll use words like 'coffee', 'please', 'thank you'"

### A2 Conversation: Restaurant Ordering (15-minute session)

**Scenario**: You're a waiter at a traditional restaurant; learner is a customer

**Your role**: Attentive waiter
**Learner role**: Hungry customer with limited Spanish

**Setup** (1 minute):
- "I'm a waiter at a Spanish restaurant. You want to order lunch. I'll give you choices, speak clearly, and help if you get stuck. You'll use words we learned yesterday: chicken, fish, water, wine. Ready?"

**Island of safety**:
- "Can you repeat?"
- "Speak slowly"
- "I want ___" [point to menu]
- "What is this?" [point to menu item]

**Conversation flow** (8 minutes):

YOU: "¡Bienvenido! What can I get you?" [Shows menu]
LEARNER: "I want..." [Points or speaks]
YOU: "Chicken? Yes, good choice. With vegetables or potatoes?" [Gesture to options]
LEARNER: "Vegetables, please" or "Vegetables"
YOU: "Perfect. And to drink?"
LEARNER: "Water" or "Water, please"
YOU: "One water. And after eating, would you like dessert?" [Gesture to dessert options]
LEARNER: "Yes" or "Yes, please" or "No, thank you"
YOU: "Great! One chicken with vegetables, water, and [dessert]. Enjoy!" [mime eating]

Mid-conversation: If learner struggles, provide word: "You want...?" and give options. If they get stuck, ask in English: "Do you want water or wine?"

**Active practice** (3 minutes):
Repeat ordering with different choices:
YOU: [Return as waiter] "What can I get you?"
LEARNER: [Should use slightly more complex language this time]

**Feedback** (2 minutes):
Positive: "Excellent! You ordered a complete meal. You said 'please' naturally. You pronounced 'verduras' perfectly!"
Improvement: "Next time, try saying your choices as a complete sentence: 'I want chicken with vegetables and water.' That helps waiters understand better."

**Reflection** (1 minute):
"Did you feel more confident this time? What do you want to practice next? Shopping? Directions?"

### B1 Conversation: Job Interview (20-minute session)

**Scenario**: You're an HR manager interviewing learner for a teaching position

**Your role**: Professional but friendly HR manager
**Learner role**: Job candidate

**Setup** (1.5 minutes):
- "I'm interviewing you for an English teaching position at a local school. This will feel real—I'll ask about your experience, why you want the job, what you can offer. You can ask me questions too. Speak naturally. I'll correct gently. Ready?"

**Island of safety**:
- "I don't understand the question. Can you rephrase?"
- "How do you say _____ in [language]?"
- "Can you give me a moment?" [pause to think]

**Conversation flow** (15 minutes):

YOU: "Thank you for coming. Tell me about your teaching experience."
LEARNER: [Expected: 2-3 sentence response about background]
YOU: "Interesting. Why do you want to teach at our school specifically?"
LEARNER: [Expected: reason with some detail]
YOU: "What are your strengths as a teacher?"
LEARNER: [Expected: 2 strengths with examples]
YOU: "I see. How would you handle a difficult student?" [Listening for problem-solving language]
LEARNER: [Expected: scenario/approach]
YOU: "Good. Do you have questions for me?"
LEARNER: [Expected: learner asks 1-2 questions - shows engagement]
YOU: "Excellent. We'll be in touch."

Mid-conversation: If learner struggles with complex question, rephrase: "Your teaching style—what is it? Traditional? Modern? How?"

**Error handling**: Learner says grammatically incorrect sentence with good idea. Recast: "Right, so you believe communication is important in the classroom. Exactly. That's a strong approach."

**Feedback** (3 minutes):
- "You stayed professional throughout. You answered all questions. You asked a strong follow-up question."
- "You used conditional: 'I would handle this by...' Excellent advanced structure!"
- "Next time, expand answers: instead of 'I have 5 years experience', try 'I've taught for 5 years at three different schools, mostly teenagers.'"

**Reflection** (1.5 minutes):
- "How comfortable were you?"
- "Did that feel like a real interview?"
- "What topic do you want to practice next?"

### B2 Conversation: Debate on Social Topic (25-minute session)

**Scenario**: Friendly debate about remote work

**Your role**: Informed opponent with mild disagreement
**Learner role**: Advocate for position

**Setup** (1.5 minutes):

### B1 Cultural Deep Dive: Concept of "You" - Tu/Vous (15-minute session)

**Topic**: Why and when to use formal vs. informal "you"

**Structure**:

1. **Context** (2 min):

2. **Language exploration** (5 min):

   - Language: How do greetings change with formality?

4. **Reflection** (2 min):
   - "English lost this distinction. What did it gain? What did it lose?"

### B2-C1 Cultural Deep Dive: Politeness and Directness across Cultures (20-minute session)

**Topic**: How cultures differ in directness vs. indirectness of communication

**Structure**:

1. **Context** (2 min):
   - "Cultures arrange themselves on spectrum from direct (Germans, Dutch) to indirect (Japanese, Thai)"
   - "Language reflects and reinforces these values"

2. **Language exploration** (6 min):
   - Direct (German): "Das ist nicht gut" (This is not good) - states fact
   - Indirect (French): "C'est peut-être pas exactement ce qu'on attendait" (Perhaps this isn't exactly what we expected) - softens criticism
   - English: middle ground—we soften with "I think" or "maybe"
   - Japanese: even more indirect—criticism expressed through silence, understatement
   - Linguistic devices: hedging ("might," "perhaps"), modal softeners, subordination
   - Impact: How does phrasing change relationship dynamics?

3. **Cultural practice** (8 min):
   - Practice: Same message in different directness levels
     - Direct: "Your proposal has problems"
     - Indirect: "Your proposal has some interesting points. Perhaps we could explore some additional angles..."
   - Role-play: Giving critical feedback to boss in direct language vs. indirect language
   - Discuss: When is directness appropriate? When is indirectness necessary?
   - Negotiation exercise: Using appropriate directness for culture/context

4. **Reflection** (4 min):
   - "How might cultural miscommunication happen?"
   - "Is one way of being polite 'better'?"
   - "How do you code-switch between cultures?"

### C1-C2 Cultural Deep Dive: Untranslatable Concepts and Philosophy (25-minute session)

**Topic**: Exploring concepts that can't be translated because they embody cultural philosophy

**Structure**:

1. **Context** (2 min):
   - "Some words are untranslatable because they express a whole worldview"
   - "Understanding these teaches us how language shapes thought"

2. **Language exploration** (8 min):
   - Japanese "MONO NO AWARE" (the pathos of things) - beauty in transience, melancholy aesthetic
   - German "SCHADENFREUDE" - joy in another's misfortune
   - Spanish "SOBREMESA" (extended time at table after meal, implicit: relationships matter more than efficiency)
   - French "JE NE SAIS QUOI" (I don't know what) - indefinable quality
   - Portuguese "SAUDADE" - deep longing, nostalgia, almost spiritual melancholy
   - Mandarin "GUANXI" - relationship networks, implicit obligations
   - Analysis: What does each concept reveal about cultural values?

3. **Cultural practice** (10 min):
   - Discuss: How do these concepts shape behavior?
     - Mono no aware explains Japanese aesthetic (gardens, poetry, painting)
     - Saudade explains Portuguese emotional expression
     - Guanxi explains Asian business relationships
   - Create equivalents: Can you describe your culture's untranslatable concept?
   - Linguistic deep-dive: Why is concept untranslatable? What would it take to translate?
   - Experience: Watch short film clip, identify the untranslatable concept reflected

4. **Synthesis** (5 min):
   - Learner writes philosophical reflection: "How does my native language's untranslatable concept shape how I think?"
   - Or: learner explores how adopting another language changes your perspective
