# The Penguins' Fun March

**An exciting story set in Antarctica**

A long, long time ago, penguins lived in Antarctica. Antarctica is a very cold and snowy place. Penguins love the cold weather. This is because they have a lot of fur and fat on their bodies, so they don't feel the cold much. Penguins live together in groups to keep each other warm. The penguin village was always lively and fun. A baby penguin named Pororo lived in the penguin village. Pororo was a curious and mischievous penguin. Every day, Pororo wanted to learn something new. One day, Pororo asked his mother, "Mom, why do we always live in the same place? What else is out there?"

Mother penguin answered with a smile. "Pororo, we penguins go on a long journey every year. We move to a special place to lay eggs and raise our chicks." Pororo asked with sparkling eyes, "Really? Where are we going?" Mother penguin said, "It’s a place very far away. We have to walk all the way there." The next morning, the penguins began their journey. The penguins walked slowly in a line. This procession is called the Penguins’ March. Pororo was so excited because it was his first long trip. Pororo walked with his friend Crong, who waddled along. "Crong, let’s go fast!" Pororo said. Crong replied, "Crong crong!" and followed Pororo.

The penguins walked along snow-covered paths. Some penguins fell, and some slipped. However, the penguins kept walking while helping each other. Pororo felt tired from walking and wanted to rest for a while. But Mother penguin encouraged him, "Pororo, cheer up just a little more! We will arrive soon." During the trip, the penguins faced various difficulties. Suddenly, a blizzard blew, making it hard to see ahead. The penguins held each other's shoulders to make sure they didn't get lost. Also, there was a penguin who almost fell into the sea when the ice broke.

Fortunately, other penguins quickly rescued them, so they were safe. Pororo was worried, but he could find courage because he was with his friends. At night, the penguins gathered together to sleep. They leaned on each other’s backs to avoid the cold. Pororo was amazed to see the stars in the night sky. "Wow, there are so many stars!" Pororo said. Crong agreed by saying "Crong crong!" next to him. The penguins continued walking for several days. Finally, they arrived at their destination. It was a wide land where countless penguins gathered. The penguins greeted and welcomed each other. Pororo met many new friends.

Pororo had a great time running and sliding on the snow with his friends. The penguins began to lay eggs and raise their chicks there. Pororo’s mother also laid an egg. Pororo stayed by his mother’s side and kept the egg warm. Pororo was as happy as if he had become a father himself. One day, a small baby penguin was born from the egg. Pororo was so happy to see the baby penguin. Pororo and his friends took care of the baby penguins so they could grow up healthy. The baby penguins waddled along after Pororo and his friends. Pororo told the babies funny stories and sang songs. The baby penguins liked Pororo very much.

As time passed, the baby penguins grew bigger and bigger. Now, the baby penguins could catch fish on their own. Pororo was proud of the baby penguins. Together, the penguins overcame the winter and raised the chicks well. The penguin village became a lively place once again. Quite some time passed. Warm spring arrived. The ice in Antarctica began to melt. The penguins had to prepare for their journey again. Pororo was now an adult penguin. Pororo said to the baby penguins, "Everyone, now we have to go back home." The baby penguins were sad. But Pororo said, "Don't worry. We can come back here next year."

The penguins began to walk in a line again. This time, the baby penguins were with them too. It was so cute to see the baby penguins waddling along. Pororo encouraged the baby penguins so they wouldn't get tired. "Cheer up just a little more! We’re almost there!" The penguins walked on the snow-covered paths again. This trip was harder than the last one because of the baby penguins. The babies often fell and even got lost. However, Pororo and the other penguins took good care of them. The penguins continued their journey while helping each other. One day, the penguins came across a large river.

The river water was so cold that it was difficult to cross. Pororo was worried. "How can we cross the river?" Just then, one penguin had a great idea. "Let's all band together and make a raft!" The penguins all worked together to make a raft. They put the baby penguins on the raft, and the adult penguins pulled it. The penguins crossed the river after a difficult process. Pororo was proud of the penguins. For several days, they kept walking. Finally, the penguins returned home. The penguin village was filled with penguins. They welcomed each other warmly. Pororo met his mother. She gave him a hug. "Pororo, you did so well! I'm so glad you returned safely!"

Pororo told his mother everything that had happened. Pororo had a fun time with the baby penguins. He caught fish for them and told them interesting stories. The baby penguins liked Pororo very much. Pororo had now become a wonderful father penguin. Every year, Pororo went on long journeys with the penguins. Leading the Penguins’ March, Pororo learned and experienced new things. Pororo gave the penguins courage and hope. The penguins respected and followed him. They learned how to live together and lived happily while loving one another. The Penguins’ March continued every year.

The penguins showed the beautiful sight of overcoming difficulties and living while helping each other. Pororo’s story was passed down through the penguin village. Pororo became a hero forever remembered by the penguins. This story tells us something important: that we can overcome any difficulty if we work together. And how precious it is to love and help each other. Like the penguins, we too must try to live happily while cherishing and loving one another. The Penguins’ March is not just a simple move; it is a beautiful journey that shows the importance of family, love, and community.
